# Presidio Heights

The smallest and quietest of the northside mansion neighborhoods: a post-1906 grid of
architect-designed houses (Maybeck, Julia Morgan, Arthur Brown Jr.) squeezed between the
Presidio Wall and California Street, with Sacramento Street's boutique row and the Laurel
Village shops as its only commerce. Anchored on its architecture and its two great
oddities: a 150-foot Byzantine dome and the private street that got sold at a tax auction.

## Candidates

### 1. Storm the Maybeck Castle
- **Do:** Find the Roos House — Bernard Maybeck's 1909 black-and-white half-timbered
  Tudor "castle," SF Landmark #56 — at 3500 Jackson St (corner of Locust). From the
  public sidewalk, every teammate strikes a royal pose (invisible crown, knighting
  gesture, or curtsy) with the half-timbered facade behind you. It's a private home:
  stay on the sidewalk, keep it quick and quiet.
- **Where:** 3500 Jackson St at Locust St
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Performance
- **Photo:** The whole team mid-royal-pose with the Tudor half-timbering and gables
  clearly visible behind them.
- **Why here:** Presidio Heights is a museum of architect-built mansions, and Maybeck's
  medieval fantasy is its crown jewel.

### 2. Buy Back the Street
- **Do:** Walk to the gated entrance of Presidio Terrace, San Francisco's most exclusive
  private street — sold in its entirety at a 2017 city tax auction for $90,100. On paper
  you brought (or scrounged), hand-letter a sign reading "SOLD! $90,100" and photograph
  the team holding it in front of the gate. Stay on the public sidewalk; do not enter or
  block the driveway.
- **Where:** Presidio Terrace gate, Arguello Blvd at Washington St.
- **Time:** 5–10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Performance
- **Photo:** The team and the SOLD sign at the gate of a billionaires' street.
- **Why here:** A private street that briefly belonged to a couple who paid about what
  a used car costs.

### 3. Dome Peekaboo
- **Do:** Capture Temple Emanu-El's 150-foot Byzantine dome twice: (a) a close-up from
  the sidewalk at Lake St and Arguello Blvd with the dome filling the frame and at least
  one teammate in the shot, and (b) a "peekaboo" shot taken from a spot inside the
  neighborhood at least 3 blocks away where the dome pokes above rooftops or trees
  (hunt the westward sightlines along Washington, Jackson, or Clay). Both photos
  required; the dome must be identifiable in each.
- **Where:** Temple Emanu-El, Lake St at Arguello Blvd, plus your found vantage point
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Frame the shot
- **Photo:** The pair: dome towering overhead up close, then peeking over Presidio
  Heights rooftops from blocks away.
- **Why here:** Arthur Brown Jr. (architect of City Hall) gave the neighborhood a
  Hagia Sophia-inspired dome in 1926, and it photobombs the skyline from all over
  these hills.

### 4. Cupcakes Over the Cemetery
- **Do:** At SusieCakes in Laurel Village, buy one cupcake (any flavor). Before anyone
  takes a bite, tell your team where they're standing: Laurel Village was built on
  Laurel Hill Cemetery, where tens of thousands of 19th-century San Franciscans were
  buried before the graves were moved to Colma. Then a teammate eats the first bite —
  frosting mustache encouraged — under the shop sign. Completion = cupcake purchased
  and first bite photographed on site.
- **Where:** SusieCakes, 3509 California St (Laurel Village; open Sat–Sun 10am–7pm)
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** ~$6
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Eat & buy
- **Photo:** Teammate mid-first-bite (or sporting a frosting mustache) under the
  SusieCakes sign.
- **Why here:** The neighborhood's cheerful little main-street row sits directly on top
  of a vanished Gold Rush-era cemetery — sweetest haunted snack in the game.

### 5. Over the Wall, Down the Slide
- **Do:** At West Pacific Ave and Spruce St, find the old stone Presidio Wall — the
  boundary that has separated the city from the Army post since the 1890s — and have
  every teammate touch it. Then pass through into Julius Kahn Playground and every
  teammate rides the big slide. Completion = all teammates slid.
- **Where:** Presidio Wall and Julius Kahn Playground, West Pacific Ave at Spruce St
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Body
- **Photo:** The last teammate mid-slide with the rest of the team waving at the bottom.
- **Why here:** This playground on the neighborhood's doorstep is named for Congressman
  Julius Kahn, who fought to keep the Presidio public land — sliding here is literally
  enjoying what he saved, one step over the neighborhood's namesake wall.

### 6. Sacramento Street Window Safari
- **Do:** On Sacramento St between Spruce St and Presidio Ave, photograph 5 different
  storefront windows, each containing a different item from this list: (1) a chair or
  lamp, (2) a toy or stuffed animal, (3) fresh flowers or a live plant, (4) an article
  of clothing, (5) a book or picture frame. One item per storefront, five distinct
  storefronts, a teammate pointing at the item in every shot. Works from the sidewalk
  even when the shops are closed.
- **Where:** Sacramento St, 3300–3700 blocks (Presidio Ave to Spruce St)
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** A five-shot set of teammates pointing out finds in the boutique windows.
- **Why here:** Sacramento Street's stretch of design shops and boutiques is
  the neighborhood's whole downtown — this is how locals actually experience it.

## Recommended trio

**3, 5, 6** — Hunt the Temple Emanu-El dome up close and then peeking over rooftops
three blocks away, touch the old Presidio Wall before sliding down Julius Kahn's big
slide (the pure-fun entry), and play window bingo along Sacramento Street. Two finds and
a slide; nothing failable, zero strangers, all $0, ≈ 30–40 minutes.
