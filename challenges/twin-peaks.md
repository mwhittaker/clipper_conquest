# Twin Peaks

Twin Peaks is the roof of the game board: two grassy ~920-foot summits — Eureka (north)
and Noe (south), the Spanish called them "Los Pechos de la Chola" — with a 360° view,
a famously windy saddle, and Sutro Tower's three-pronged silhouette looming one ridge
over (the tower's base sits inside this neighborhood too). The eastern half of the
figure-eight roadway is permanently car-free, so you can roam the loop and the summit
stair-paths safely; fog isn't a bug here, it's the boss battle. (Boundary check against
the official Analysis Neighborhoods dataset: both summits, Christmas Tree Point, and
Sutro Tower are in; Tank Hill is in Inner Sunset and the Pemberton steps are in
Castro/Upper Market, so they're off-limits for this tile.)

## Candidates

### 1. Semaphore on the Twins
- **Do:** Split your team in two. One group climbs the stair-path to the top of Eureka
  Peak (north), the other to Noe Peak (south) — they're only ~660 feet apart across the
  saddle. The Noe group generates a random letter (any random-letter site) and forms it with
  their bodies (arms, legs, one person or a pile of people — a Y, a T, an X, a 7...). The
  Eureka group reads it across the gap and texts ONE answer. Right = swap: Eureka
  forms one, Noe reads it. Complete when both peaks have read one correctly. Wrong = new
  letter. Team of two: one person per peak, one letter each.
- **Where:** Eureka and Noe summit paths off Twin Peaks Blvd, uphill from the Christmas
  Tree Point parking area.
- **Time:** ~15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (new letter until it's read)
- **Type:** Secret message
- **Photo:** Zoomed from one summit: the other half of the team spelling their letter
  against the sky.
- **Why here:** It's the only neighborhood where your team can literally occupy both
  eponymous peaks at once — and signal across.

### 2. Bridge or No Bridge?
- **Do:** Before anyone can see north — standing in the Christmas Tree Point parking
  area facing the hillside — the team writes one word on a phone screen: "BRIDGE" or
  "NO BRIDGE," predicting whether at least one Golden Gate Bridge tower will be visible
  when you reach the Eureka Peak summit. Photograph the written call, then climb. You
  win only if reality matches your call at the moment you summit. One shot, no take-backs.
- **Where:** Christmas Tree Point lot (call), Eureka Peak summit (verdict).
- **Time:** ~10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** one-shot
- **Type:** Call your shot
- **Photo:** Two-photo proof: the pre-climb phone screen with the call, then the summit
  panorama toward the Golden Gate showing bridge or fog — team pointing either way.
- **Why here:** Twin Peaks is the city's fog gauge; gambling on the marine layer is the
  most Twin Peaks thing you can do.

### 3. Blind Summit
- **Do:** At the base of the stair-path up Eureka Peak (north), one teammate closes their
  eyes (or ties a scarf over them). The rest guide them to the summit marker using
  VOICE ONLY — no touching, no holding hands. A step off the path or a hand on the
  blind climber = back to the base. Complete when the blind climber touches the summit
  marker; they open their eyes to the view. Pick a calm day and go slow.
- **Where:** Eureka Peak stair-path off Twin Peaks Blvd, uphill from the Christmas Tree
  Point parking area.
- **Time:** ~10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (back to the base and retry)
- **Type:** Secret message
- **Photo:** The blind climber's hand on the marker, eyes just opening, city behind.
- **Why here:** The classic Twin Peaks reveal — the whole city appearing at once — is
  better when you've earned it blind.

### 4. The Market Street Arrow
- **Do:** From the Christmas Tree Point overlook, find the one spot where Market Street
  runs as a single straight line from the bottom of your frame all the way to the bay,
  and photograph it — the street must be readable as one continuous stripe pointing at
  the Ferry Building/waterfront. If the fog has eaten downtown, you may mark this failed
  and re-attempt any time you're back in the neighborhood.
- **Where:** Christmas Tree Point overlook railing, just below Eureka Peak.
- **Time:** ~5–10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** retryable (an attempt = one visit's try; fog-out = failed attempt)
- **Type:** Frame the shot
- **Photo:** The classic postcard nobody frames right: Market Street as a perfect arrow
  from your feet to the water.
- **Why here:** Market Street was platted to aim straight at Twin Peaks — this is the
  only place in SF where you can look down the barrel.

### 5. Habitat Bingo
- **Do:** The summit grasslands are protected habitat for the endangered Mission blue
  butterfly. Staying on trails, first find any posted sign that mentions the Mission
  blue or habitat restoration. Then, in the grassland along the summit loop, spot and
  photograph THREE different living things that aren't people or dogs — any butterfly,
  bee, beetle, bird, lizard, or a flower in bloom. Complete with the sign plus three
  creatures/blooms.
- **Where:** Trailheads and paths of the Twin Peaks Natural Area, around the summit loop
  and Christmas Tree Point.
- **Time:** ~10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (keep looking along the loop)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** The sign, then the three finds in close-up.
- **Why here:** These 31 acres are one of the last city strongholds of a butterfly that
  exists almost nowhere else on Earth.

### 6. Lap the Car-Free Eight
- **Do:** Walk the entire permanently car-free eastern arm of the Twin Peaks Blvd
  figure eight — barrier to barrier, no shortcuts across the slope (stay on the
  roadway/promenade). Take a photo of the team touching the road-closure barrier or
  gate at the start, and another at the far barrier. Both photos = complete.
- **Where:** Twin Peaks Blvd, east side of the figure eight (closed to cars since 2016);
  gates at the north and south ends of the closed section, ~0.6 miles apart.
- **Time:** ~15–20 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Go there
- **Photo:** Bookend shots — team hands on the closure gates at each end, empty
  cliff-edge boulevard curving away behind.
- **Why here:** SF gave walkers an entire mountaintop boulevard to walk down the middle of;
  it deserves a victory lap.

## Recommended trio

**1, 2, 4** — Signal letters between the two summits with your bodies, bet BRIDGE or
NO BRIDGE before you can see north (the neighborhood's one-shot), and find the one spot
where Market Street runs as a single straight stripe to the bay. A signaling game, a
called shot, a find; one one-shot, one fog-retryable, zero strangers, all $0, ≈ 35
minutes on foot.
