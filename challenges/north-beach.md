# North Beach

The official Analysis Neighborhood is far bigger than "Little Italy": it runs from the
Beat-era corner of Columbus & Broadway (City Lights, Jack Kerouac Alley) through
Washington Square and over Telegraph Hill — Coit Tower, the wooden Filbert Steps, the
wild parrots — all the way to the waterfront, where it swallows Fisherman's Wharf,
Pier 45's Musée Mécanique, and the sea lions of Pier 39. Anchors below span all three
zones: piazza, hill, and pier.

## Candidates

### 1. Sal's Orchestra
- **Do:** The Musée Mécanique (free entry) holds 300+ antique coin-operated machines,
  announced by Laffing Sal, the cackling 1930s funhouse giantess. Your task: with at
  most $3 in quarters per attempt, get THREE machines visibly running AT THE SAME TIME,
  captured in one continuous video pan — teammates fan out, coins drop in sequence,
  timing is everything (most machines run well under a minute). Sal counts as a machine.
  Blow the budget with no triple = attempt over; change machine is on site, try again.
- **Where:** Musée Mécanique, Pier 45 Shed A, foot of Taylor St (weekends 10am–8pm)
- **Time:** 10–20 min | **Cost:** ~$1–3 per attempt
- **Failable:** retryable ($3 of quarters = one attempt)
- **Type:** One of a kind
- **Photo:** Frame-grab of the triple: three antique machines mid-whir in one shot,
  conductor's arms raised.
- **Why here:** One of the world's largest antique arcade collections lives free on a
  pier — it was always begging to be played as an orchestra.

### 2. The K-Dock Accord
- **Do:** At Pier 39's K-Dock, the sea lions have squatted rent-free since 1989. Pick
  two counters. Standing apart at the rail, each silently counts every sea lion hauled
  out on the floats — no talking, no pointing, no comparing, up to three minutes each.
  Both write their number down hidden, then reveal at the same moment. The two counts
  must MATCH EXACTLY. They do = complete. They don't = failed. One try, ever.
- **Where:** Pier 39, west side marina (K-Dock viewing area — free, 24/7)
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** one-shot
- **Type:** Mind meld
- **Photo:** The simultaneous reveal — two hands, two numbers, and the raft of judges
  lounging behind (matching or heartbreakingly off by one).
- **Why here:** The famous freeloaders will not hold still while you count — getting
  two humans to agree about them is the real negotiation.

### 3. Get to Coit Tower
- **Do:** Get to Coit Tower. That's it — the hill is the challenge. Any route counts:
  the wooden Filbert Steps through the Grace Marchant Garden (recommended, and listen
  for the wild parrots), the Greenwich Steps, or the road if you must. Complete when
  the whole team stands at the tower's base.
- **Where:** Coit Tower, 1 Telegraph Hill Blvd, atop Telegraph Hill
- **Time:** 15–20 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Go there
- **Photo:** The team at the tower's fluted base, city and bay falling away behind.
- **Why here:** Lillie Coit's 1933 tower is the neighborhood's exclamation point, and
  every way up Telegraph Hill earns it — this is the "the place is the game" entry.

### 4. Crime Scene at Coit Tower
- **Do:** Enter Coit Tower's free ground-floor mural lobby (no elevator ticket needed —
  the paid ride to the top is skippable and the historic elevator is often down
  anyway). In Victor Arnautoff's 1934 fresco "City Life," find three painted details:
  (a) the robbery in progress in the lower right, (b) the car accident in the center,
  and (c) the newsstand with its rack of periodicals. Complete when all three are
  found and photographed.
- **Where:** Coit Tower lobby, 1 Telegraph Hill Blvd (lobby free, open daily from
  10am)
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** A teammate pointing at the painted holdup while making their best
  shocked-witness face.
- **Why here:** Depression-era WPA frescoes wrap Coit Tower's base, and Arnautoff hid
  an entire crime blotter inside his street scene — a free museum most tourists ride
  right past.

### 5. The Language of the Birds
- **Do:** At the corner of Broadway & Columbus, twenty-three sculpted books hang frozen
  in flight overhead — "Language of the Birds." Your task: photograph a REAL, live bird
  sharing the scene — perched on or flying among the book sculptures, or sitting on the
  wires and fixtures immediately around them. Complete when your photo shows at least
  one actual bird with the books in frame.
- **Where:** Language of the Birds plaza, northwest corner of Broadway & Columbus Ave
  (beside City Lights); outdoors, 24/7
- **Time:** 5–20 min (pigeon-dependent) | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (patience — this corner is rarely pigeonless for long)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** A live bird upstaging the sculpture it inspired, flock of books overhead.
- **Why here:** A public artwork about birds, judged complete only by an actual bird —
  the Beats would have approved of the collaboration.

### 6. Old-Timey Water with Ben Franklin
- **Do:** In Washington Square, find the 1879 Benjamin Franklin statue and complete
  three finds: (a) the old spigot labels on its temperance-fountain base (donor Henry
  Cogswell thought free water would beat demon drink), (b) the inscription about the
  time capsule buried beneath — to be reopened in 2079, and (c) the Volunteer Firemen
  memorial nearby, where teammates copy the bronze firefighters' exact poses. Complete
  when all three are photographed with the twin spires of Saints Peter & Paul Church
  in at least one shot.
- **Where:** Washington Square, Columbus Ave & Filbert St/Union St
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** Teammates frozen in firefighter poses beside the bronze crew, church
  spires rising behind.
- **Why here:** North Beach's living-room lawn hides a buried time capsule, a
  temperance fountain, and a statue paid for by Lillie Hitchcock Coit — the firefighter
  superfan whose tower watches from the hill above.

## Recommended trio

**1, 2, 3** — Pier to pier to summit: conduct the antique arcade on Pier 45, then the
one-shot K-Dock Accord (two silent counters, one chance to match), then get yourselves
up Telegraph Hill to Coit Tower — the trio's pure "the place is the game" entry. One
coordination game, one high-stakes agreement, one climb; exactly one one-shot, zero
strangers, ~$3 in quarters, ≈ 40–55 minutes.
