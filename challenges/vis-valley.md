# Visitacion Valley

SF's sunny southeastern valley, where a volunteer-built "ladder" of six themed community
gardens — the Visitacion Valley Greenway — climbs the hillside block by block from Leland
Avenue up to Tioga Avenue, its parcels linked by whimsical wrought-iron gates (8 by
sculptor Jim Growden, 2 by Fran Martin) celebrating local plants and critters. Leland
Avenue below is SF's most small-town main street (library, bakeries, corner shops), the
T Third streetcar ends its run here at Sunnydale, and tucked by the old Schlage Lock
works sits the storybook enclave of Little Hollywood. Anchors: the Greenway ladder,
Leland Ave, the T terminus, Schlage's 1926 office building, and Little Hollywood.
(McLaren Park is a separate neighborhood and is not used.)

## Candidates

### 1. Climb the Garden Ladder
- **Do:** The Visitacion Valley Greenway is a ladder of six block-long gardens. Start at
  Hans Schiller Plaza on Leland Ave and climb one garden at a time — crossing Raymond,
  Arleta, Teddy, Campbell and Tucker — to the Native Plant Garden at Tioga Ave. At each
  garden, spot one decorative ironwork detail (animal, plant, or vegetable) worked into
  its gate or fence. At the top, a chosen teammate must recite all six details in order,
  bottom to top, from memory. Mistake = start the recital over (you may walk back down to
  re-check). Gardens may be locked; the gates and fences are on the public sidewalk.
- **Where:** Visitacion Valley Greenway, Leland Ave up to Tioga Ave.
- **Time:** 20 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (re-check and re-recite)
- **Type:** Memory
- **Photo:** The reciter at the top garden, eyes closed, counting off six fingers.
- **Why here:** Neighbors turned six vacant lots into a stairway of gardens — each with
  its own iron signature.

### 2. Picnic at the Mosaic Columns
- **Do:** Buy any snack or drink (≤$10 total) from an open storefront on Leland Ave
  between Bayshore Blvd and Rutland St, then eat it sitting at Hans Schiller Plaza — on
  the benches under the pergola or by the low mosaic wall. Complete when the team photo
  shows the treat, a teammate mid-bite, and at least one of the plaza's three tiled
  columns.
- **Where:** Leland Ave commercial strip; Hans Schiller Plaza at Leland Ave near Rutland St
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** ~$5–10
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Eat & buy
- **Photo:** Team snacking under the pergola with the whimsical mosaic columns in frame
- **Why here:** Leland is the little main street "outsiders never visit" — being the
  outsiders who do, then picnicking in the plaza its neighbors tiled by hand, is pure
  Vis Valley.

### 3. One Car or Two?
- **Do:** Get to Sunnydale Station (Bayshore Blvd at Sunnydale Ave), the final stop of
  the T Third and the southernmost Muni Metro stop in San Francisco. Muni Metro trains
  run as one car or two coupled cars. Before the next arriving train is close enough to
  count, the team calls it out loud on video: ONE or TWO. Right = complete. Wrong = new
  call for the next train (every 10–12 minutes).
- **Where:** Sunnydale Muni Station platform, Bayshore Blvd & Sunnydale Ave (no boarding
  required)
- **Time:** 5–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (one call per train)
- **Type:** Call your shot
- **Photo:** The team on the platform pointing at the verdict train, "Sunnydale" sign in
  frame.
- **Why here:** Vis Valley is where SF's rail system runs out of city — the one place in
  the game you can call a train at the very end of the line.

### 4. The New Arrivals Wager
- **Do:** Inside the Visitacion Valley Branch Library, before anyone finds it, the team
  agrees on ONE guess: how many books are on the New Arrivals display shelf right now?
  Write it down, then find the shelf and count together in library voices. Within 3 =
  complete. Off? One more guess, on the hold shelf, and count that. Miss twice and the library
  wins — mark it failed. While you're there, look up: "Full Circles," Mark Grieve's sculpture of steel hoops and
  bicycle gears, hangs from the dome.
- **Where:** Visitacion Valley Branch Library, 201 Leland Ave (open Sat & Sun daytime)
- **Time:** 5–10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** retryable (two guesses total: New Arrivals, then the hold shelf)
- **Type:** Guess, then measure
- **Photo:** The guess held up beneath the hoops, shelf in the background.
- **Why here:** The 2011 library became Leland Ave's proudest building — and its New
  Arrivals shelf changes every day, so nobody can know the number in advance.

### 5. Silent Movie in Little Hollywood
- **Do:** Go to Little Hollywood Park in the 1920s storybook enclave named for its
  movie-set houses, and shoot a silent movie: one teammate, the director, generates a
  random number 1–10 on a phone and whispers that scene to the actor — 1 brushing teeth,
  2 walking a dog, 3 making a sandwich, 4 catching a fish, 5 changing a tire, 6
  parallel parking, 7 milking a cow, 8 flying a kite, 9 shoveling snow, 10 juggling
  (re-roll repeats). The actor performs it with no sound and
  no props. The rest of the team gets ONE guess. Right = new actor, new scene. Wrong =
  the streak restarts. Complete when the team guesses three scenes in a row.
- **Where:** Little Hollywood Park, Lathrop Ave & Tocoloma Ave (east of Bayshore Blvd)
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (the streak restarts until you hit three)
- **Type:** Act it, guess it
- **Photo:** The actor mid-scene on the lawn, the storybook cottages behind.
- **Why here:** Little Hollywood is Vis Valley's oddest secret — a pocket of
  glamour-styled cottages that was always waiting for its silent-film era.

### 6. Count the Keys
- **Do:** In front of the 1926 brick Schlage Lock office building — sole survivor of the
  lock factory that ran Vis Valley's economy for 70 years — every teammate secretly
  writes down a guess: how many keys is the WHOLE team carrying right now? No peeking in
  pockets. Then everyone empties their keys into a pile and you count (a fob or car key
  counts as one). Anyone within 2 of the true count = complete. Nobody within 2 =
  failed. One count, ever.
- **Where:** Schlage Lock building, Bayshore Blvd near Blanken Ave.
- **Time:** 5–10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** one-shot
- **Type:** Guess, then measure
- **Photo:** The pile of keys on the sidewalk, the winning guess held above it, the
  brick building behind.
- **Why here:** A lock factory's last building is the only proper place to count keys.

## Recommended trio

**1, 6, 5** — Climb the six-garden Greenway and recite its ironwork from memory, guess
the team's total key count at the old Schlage Lock building (the neighborhood's
one-shot), and act out silent-movie scenes in Little Hollywood. A memory game, a called
shot, a charades game; zero strangers, all $0, ≈ 35–45 minutes.
