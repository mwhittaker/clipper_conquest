# Potrero Hill

Sunny, village-y hill where nearly every north–south street is named for a US state,
goats once grazed the slopes (hence "Goat Hill"), and the north face drops straight
toward the downtown skyline. Anchored on: the Vermont Street switchbacks at McKinley
Square (the "other Lombard" — verified inside the official Analysis Neighborhood
boundary: the 101 freeway line crosses Vermont only at the very bottom near 22nd, so
the curves themselves are in-bounds), the 18th Street commercial strip, and the
postcard skyline views from the hilltop parks.

## Candidates

### 1. Crookeder Than Lombard?
- **Do:** Start at the top of Vermont Street's curves at McKinley Square (20th &
  Vermont, where the "trucks not advised" sign marks the drop). Walk down alongside the
  switchbacks on the sidewalk — never in the roadway — counting every hairpin turn out
  loud as a team. Stop at the LAST curve (don't continue to 22nd St under the freeway —
  that sliver is out of the neighborhood). Completion: proof photo taken, with your
  official team count held up on fingers. Fun fact to argue on the way down: the Travel
  Channel measured Vermont's sinuosity at 1.56 vs Lombard's 1.2 — locals say this WPA
  1930s street is the real crookedest street in San Francisco.
- **Where:** Vermont St between 20th St (McKinley Square) and the bottom of the curves,
  west edge of Potrero Hill
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Go there
- **Photo:** Team mid-switchback with fingers showing their hairpin count, the
  serpentine curves and tree canopy snaking downhill behind them
- **Why here:** Potrero Hill's proudest claim: its crooked street out-crookeds the
  famous one — without a single tourist bus.

### 2. State Street Sweep
- **Do:** Potrero Hill's north–south streets are almost all named for US states. Phones
  away — no maps. Photograph your team under SIX different state-named street signs
  (Texas, Missouri, Connecticut, Arkansas, Wisconsin, Carolina, Rhode Island, Kansas,
  Vermont, Utah, Mississippi, Pennsylvania...). De Haro and San Bruno don't count.
- **Where:** Along or just off the 18th Street strip.
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** Six state-sign shots; the keeper is the team pointing up at one of them.
- **Why here:** A whole hill of streets named for the states — half of them within a
  few blocks of each other.

### 3. Four Bases in a Row
- **Do:** At the Potrero Hill Recreation Center ballfield (Arkansas & 22nd), with the
  downtown skyline behind the outfield, play catch the Little League way: one teammate
  stands on the pitcher's mound and must throw a ball (bring one, or buy a cheap one on
  18th St) to a teammate standing on first, then second, then third, then home — four
  clean catches in a row. Any drop = start again from first. Team of two: the catcher
  runs to the next base between throws.
- **Where:** Potrero Hill Rec Center, 801 Arkansas St (public field; wait out any game
  in progress).
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0–5
- **Failable:** no (restart at first base)
- **Type:** Aim & throw
- **Photo:** The throw to home, Salesforce Tower standing behind the backstop.
- **Why here:** The ballfield with the best skyline backdrop in the city.

### 4. Blind Slice at Goat Hill
- **Do:** Go to Goat Hill Pizza at 18th & Connecticut (open daily from 11am) — named for
  Potrero Hill's original nickname, from the goats that grazed here. Buy two slices with
  clearly different toppings (≤$10 total). Outside, a teammate closes their eyes and is
  handed one bite of each in a secret order; they must say which slice is which. Right =
  complete. Wrong = new taster, re-shuffle — one attempt per teammate, and when the
  slices run out, so do the attempts: buy two more or mark it failed.
- **Where:** Goat Hill Pizza, 300 Connecticut St at 18th St.
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** ~$10
- **Failable:** retryable (one attempt per teammate per pair of slices)
- **Type:** Blind senses
- **Photo:** Eyes shut, mid-bite, the Goat Hill sign behind.
- **Why here:** The hill's namesake pizzeria, and the tastiest sense test in the game.

### 5. Hill Hunt at Christopher's Books
- **Do:** Inside Christopher's Books, the tiny corner bookshop at 18th & Missouri, find
  a book with the word "Hill" in its title. No phones, no asking the staff — shelf logic
  only.
- **Where:** Christopher's Books, 1400 18th St at Missouri (open Sat 10–8, Sun 10–6).
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** The book held up at the shop's corner entrance.
- **Why here:** A one-room neighborhood treasure on the hill — browse politely.

### 6. Door Count at Farley's
- **Do:** At Farley's (1315 18th St, "Serving Coffee & Community" since 1989), buy any
  drink — hot chocolate or steamed milk counts (~$5) — and take a seat
  with a view of the door. Before the clock starts, the team agrees on ONE guess: how
  many people will walk in through that door in the next TEN minutes? Count together.
  Within 2 = complete. Off? Call the next ten.
- **Where:** Farley's, 1315 18th St (between Missouri & Texas).
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** ~$5
- **Failable:** no (call the next ten minutes)
- **Type:** Call your shot
- **Photo:** The tally sheet beside the drink, the door in the background.
- **Why here:** Potrero's 1989 coffeehouse is the hill's living room, and nobody knows
  how full it gets on a Saturday until they sit and count.

## Recommended trio

**1, 2, 3** — Count Vermont Street's hairpins as a team, find six state-named street
signs with no map, and throw four clean catches around the Rec Center bases under the
skyline. A count, a no-phone hunt, a throwing game; nothing failable, zero strangers,
$0–5, ≈ 35–45 minutes. (The "Hill" book hunt at Christopher's stays the alternate.)
