# Japantown

One of only three remaining Japantowns in the United States, packed into a few blocks
around the Peace Pagoda: the Japan Center malls (Kinokuniya's books, Daiso, capsule-toy
walls, dessert counters) hum on weekends, the Buchanan Mall pedestrian block carries Ruth
Asawa's bronze origami fountains along a cobblestone "river," and a hidden brick lane of
1882 cottages (Cottage Row) survives one block north. Challenges anchor on the pagoda,
the origami tradition, the mall treasure-hunt density, and that secret lane.

## Candidates

### 1. Crane Landing
- **Do:** Buy origami paper at Paper Tree — The Origami Store (family-run here since
  1968). Pick a folder and a reader. The reader looks at a crane diagram (or phone
  tutorial); the folder may NOT look at it and must fold only from the reader's spoken
  instructions — no pointing, no touching the paper. When the crane is done, perch it on
  the low stone wall of one of Ruth Asawa's bronze Origami Fountains on the Buchanan Mall.
  A torn or failed crane = fresh sheet, try again.
- **Where:** Paper Tree, 1743 Buchanan St, then the Buchanan Mall fountains.
- **Time:** 15–20 min | **Cost:** ~$3–5
- **Failable:** no (new sheet until it's a crane)
- **Type:** Secret message
- **Photo:** The talked-through crane perched on Asawa's bronze origami fountain.
- **Why here:** A 1968 origami shop steps from Ruth Asawa's origami fountains — folding
  by voice alone is the neighborhood's own telephone game.

### 2. Hi-Chew Blind Test
- **Do:** At Nijiya Market (or any Japan Center snack shop), buy THREE different flavors
  of the same Japanese candy — Hi-Chew, Pocky, Kit Kat, whatever the shelf has in three
  flavors, ≤$8 total. Outside, one teammate closes their eyes; another unwraps one piece
  of each and hands them over in a secret order. The taster names all three flavors from
  the packaging list. Three for three = complete. A miss = new taster, new order — one
  attempt per teammate, and when the candy runs out, so do the attempts (buy more or
  mark it failed).
- **Where:** Nijiya Market, 1737 Post St, or any snack counter in the Japan Center malls.
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** ~$5–8
- **Failable:** retryable (one attempt per teammate per pack)
- **Type:** Blind senses
- **Photo:** Eyes shut, mid-chew, three wrappers fanned out beside them.
- **Why here:** Japan Center's snack aisles carry flavors that exist nowhere else in the
  city — and the wrapper is the only answer key.

### 3. Triple-Digit Treasure
- **Do:** Inside Kinokuniya Bookstore, find on the shelves a manga volume whose number
  is 100 or higher (English or Japanese section — One Piece alone is well past volume
  110). Photograph a teammate holding it with the volume number legible, then reshelve
  it neatly. No purchase required.
- **Where:** Kinokuniya, 1581 Webster St, upstairs in the Kinokuniya Building of the
  Japan Center (open weekends 10:30am–8pm).
- **Time:** 5–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (achievable with persistence)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** Teammate holding a volume with a three-digit number on the spine/cover,
  bookstore shelves behind.
- **Why here:** The largest Japanese bookstore in the city; only here do series run deep
  enough for triple digits to be findable.

### 4. The Six Little Cottages
- **Do:** Find Cottage Row — a brick-paved pedestrian lane hidden mid-block between
  Sutter and Bush (between Webster and Fillmore), a National Register historic district.
  Walk its full length and count the small matching 1882 cottages whose front doors open
  directly onto the bricks; report the count with your claim. (Referee key: six,
  addressed 1–6 Cottage Row.)
- **Where:** Cottage Row & the adjacent mini park; enter from Sutter St just east of
  Fillmore, ~3 blocks northwest of Peace Plaza.
- **Time:** 10 min incl. the walk over | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (outdoor, 24/7; recount anytime)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** The team on the brick lane, row of cottages behind, holding up fingers for
  the count.
- **Why here:** A secret Victorian lane inside the historic heart of pre-war Japantown —
  most San Franciscans have never heard of it.

### 5. Eat Something With a Face
- **Do:** In the Japan Center malls' food corridors, buy one snack or dessert shaped
  like an animal — a taiyaki fish cone at Uji Time, a fish waffle, a panda bun, anything
  with a recognizable creature form — and photograph a teammate taking a bite while the
  animal is still clearly identifiable. Complete on purchase + photo.
- **Where:** Japan Center malls, Post St between Fillmore & Laguna (e.g. Uji Time, 22
  Peace Plaza #440; malls are liveliest on weekend afternoons).
- **Time:** 10–20 min (weekend lines) | **Cost:** ~$6–8
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Eat & buy
- **Photo:** Mid-chomp portrait — teammate vs. fish (or panda), face to face.
- **Why here:** Taiyaki and animal-shaped sweets are Japan Center food-hall signatures;
  the malls are the neighborhood's weekend heartbeat.

### 6. Call Your Capsule
- **Do:** At the Gashapon Bandai Official Shop (or any capsule-machine bank in the Japan
  Center malls), pick one machine. Before turning the crank, record a short video of a
  teammate declaring the color of the capsule shell that will come out (most machines
  mix 4–5 shell colors). One turn only: if the capsule matches the called color, the
  challenge is complete; if not, it's failed — but you keep the mystery toy either way.
- **Where:** Gashapon Bandai Official Shop, Japan Center (Kinokuniya Building, 1581
  Webster St); other capsule banks throughout both malls also count.
- **Time:** 5–10 min | **Cost:** ~$5
- **Failable:** one-shot (one declared turn; win or lose, the toy is yours)
- **Type:** Pure luck
- **Photo:** The opened capsule half held next to the toy, machine wall behind — plus
  the declaration video.
- **Why here:** An official Bandai gashapon shop — walls of Japanese capsule machines —
  is a Japantown-only phenomenon in SF.

## Recommended trio

**1, 5, 6** — Fold a crane from spoken instructions only, buy and bite something with a
face in the Japan Center food halls, then call the capsule color at the gashapon wall
(the neighborhood's one-shot). One teammate-roles game, one snack, one called shot;
~$10–13 total, zero strangers, ≈ 35–45 minutes. (Daruma-san on Peace Plaza stays the
free alternate.)
