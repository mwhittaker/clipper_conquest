# Golden Gate Park

This neighborhood IS the park: a 3-mile-long, human-made wonderland (plus the Panhandle
strip trailing east) stuffed with things that shouldn't exist in a city — a live bison
herd, a 1902 Dutch windmill, a hidden garden of Shakespeare's flowers, and a 1912
menagerie carousel. The museums inside charge admission, so every candidate below anchors
on the park's free (or nearly free) oddities, spread from the Koret playground in the
east to the windmill by the ocean.

## Candidates

### 1. Menagerie Roulette
- **Do:** The Golden Gate Park Carousel (built 1912) is a *menagerie* carousel — far more
  than horses. Before anyone peeks at it, the team writes down three non-horse animals
  they bet are on it. Then buy tickets, check your guesses, and ride: challenge complete
  if at least TWO of your three written animals exist on the carousel AND every teammate
  rides (any animal). Open weekends from 10 a.m. year-round; about $2.50 a ride.
- **Where:** Koret Children's Quarter, 320 Bowling Green Dr (southeast corner of the park,
  near Kezar Dr)
- **Time:** ~15 min | **Cost:** ~$2.50/person (≈$5–10 per team)
- **Failable:** one-shot (the written guess is locked before you see the carousel; the
  ride happens either way)
- **Type:** Call your shot
- **Photo:** The handwritten guess list held up next to one of the matched animals, plus
  the team mid-ride.
- **Why here:** A century-old menagerie carousel with frogs, roosters, and goats is the
  park's east-end crown jewel — and betting on which beasts it holds turns a $1 ride into
  a coin-flip moment.

### 2. B Is for Bison
- **Do:** Yes, there are real bison in San Francisco. Two teammates become the counters:
  they walk to different spots along the paddock fence (at least 50 paces apart) and
  silently count every bison they can see — the herd wanders and hides behind hillocks
  and each other, so it's harder than it sounds. They walk back and say their numbers at
  the same time. A match = complete. A mismatch = the herd's moved; pick new spots and
  count again. (Team of two: you're both counters.)
- **Where:** Bison Paddock, along John F. Kennedy Dr just west of Spreckels Lake (west
  end of the park, near 39th Ave; the dirt path along the north side gets you closest)
- **Time:** ~10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (recount until you agree)
- **Type:** Mind meld
- **Photo:** The two counters holding up matching finger-counts with bison grazing
  behind them.
- **Why here:** A herd of bison grazing in a city park since 1891 — the single best
  "wait, that exists?" in San Francisco, and they refuse to stand still for a census.

### 3. Petal Match in the Secret Garden
- **Do:** Slip through the wrought-iron gate of the Shakespeare Garden — every plant in
  it is mentioned in Shakespeare's works. On the brick wall at the back are bronze
  plaques quoting his flower lines. Pick any flower NAMED on a plaque (violet, rose,
  daisy, thyme...), then hunt through the beds until you find that flower actually
  growing. Photograph both: the plaque line and the living plant.
- **Where:** Shakespeare Garden, off Martin Luther King Jr. Dr next to the California
  Academy of Sciences (Music Concourse area, mid-park near 9th Ave)
- **Time:** ~10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (multiple flowers are named and something is always in bloom — keep
  picking plaque flowers until one matches)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** One teammate pointing at the flower's name on the bronze plaque, another
  holding a phone/finger next to the real flower.
- **Why here:** A hidden literary garden behind an iron gate, steps from the park's
  busiest plaza — most visitors walk right past it.

### 4. Mind Meld at the Portals of the Past
- **Do:** Find the Portals of the Past: a lone marble doorway standing at the edge of
  Lloyd Lake — the entrance of a Nob Hill mansion that survived the 1906 earthquake and
  fire, moved here as a memorial. Stand in front of it in silence. No talking. Every teammate secretly writes down ONE thing they can see from where
  they stand that the doorway most reminds them of, or that looks most out of place
  beside it — one word, a real visible thing. Reveal
  together. Any two matching words = complete. No match = walk
  around to the far side of Lloyd Lake and write again from there. Three rounds, then
  it's failed.
- **Where:** Lloyd Lake, off John F. Kennedy Dr near Transverse Dr (mid-west park)
- **Time:** ~10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** retryable (three rounds, a lap of the lake between each)
- **Type:** Mind meld
- **Photo:** The matching words held up in the doorway, lake behind.
- **Why here:** A doorway that survived the city's destruction, standing alone by a lake
  — the park's most haunting oddity, and a good test of whether your team thinks alike.

### 5. Summit the Island in the Lake
- **Do:** From the Stow Lake shore, cross either bridge onto Strawberry Hill — an island
  with a mountain on it. Climb the trail past Huntington Falls (a 110-foot artificial
  waterfall) to the summit, the highest point in the park. Required checkpoints, in
  order: (1) the red-and-green Chinese Pavilion near the base (a gift from Taipei),
  (2) the waterfall, (3) the summit clearing with the stone ruins of the old Sweeny
  Observatory. Photo at each.
- **Where:** Stow Lake / Strawberry Hill, mid-park off Stow Lake Dr (enter near 19th Ave
  or from the Music Concourse side)
- **Time:** ~20 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Go there
- **Photo:** Summit shot at the ruins, plus the waterfall checkpoint with the team on the
  stone steps beside the falls.
- **Why here:** A fake mountain, on an island, in a fake lake, with a fake waterfall and
  real earthquake ruins on top — peak Golden Gate Park absurdity, and an easy climb.

### 6. Spin at the Dutch Windmill
- **Do:** Walk to the 75-foot Dutch Windmill at the park's ocean corner. Build a pinwheel
  from what you have (paper folded and pinned to a pencil or stick; bring a pin or a
  paperclip) and hold it up in the sea wind in front of the real windmill. Complete when
  your pinwheel spins on its own for 10 counted seconds without a hand touching the
  blades.
- **Where:** Dutch Windmill / Queen Wilhelmina Garden, northwest corner of the park at
  John F. Kennedy Dr & the Great Highway
- **Time:** ~15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (refold until it spins)
- **Type:** Build & make
- **Photo:** The spinning paper pinwheel with the real sails behind it, tulips in season.
- **Why here:** San Francisco keeps two full-size Dutch windmills by the ocean and almost
  nobody knows; the ocean wind that once turned them will turn yours.

## Recommended trio

**6, 3, 2** — Fold a pinwheel that spins in the sea wind at the Dutch Windmill, hunt
a plaque-to-petal match in the hidden Shakespeare Garden, then send two counters along
the fence of the Bison Paddock until their numbers agree. A build, a find, an agreement
game; all $0, no strangers; the west end and the Music Concourse are one 5 Fulton hop
apart. The carousel bet, Strawberry Hill and the Portals mind meld remain as swaps.
