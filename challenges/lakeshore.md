# Lakeshore

San Francisco's wild southwest corner: the official Lakeshore analysis neighborhood
bundles Lake Merced (a freshwater lake ringed by a 4.5-mile trail, a boathouse, and a
fishing pier), Fort Funston (the city's biggest dune field, a WWII 16-inch gun battery,
a hang glider launch deck, and roughly a thousand off-leash dogs), SFSU's campus, and
Stonestown Galleria — which since 2024 hides SF's largest arcade in a former Nordstrom
basement. Anchored on the battery-and-gliders cliffs, the lake's odd relocated
conquistador statue, and Stonestown's claw machines.

## Candidates

### 1. Dune Pictionary at the Glider Deck
- **Do:** From the Fort Funston main parking lot, walk to the wooden hang glider viewing
  deck at the cliff edge, then step onto the open sand beside it. Play Pictionary in the
  dune: one teammate, the artist, generates a random number 1–8 on a phone and draws that
  item from this list — 1 dog, 2 hang glider, 3 wave, 4 bunker, 5 gull, 6 surfer, 7
  lighthouse, 8 crab — in the sand with a finger or stick (re-roll repeats). No letters, no numbers, no talking. The team gets three guesses. Right = the
  next teammate draws. Wrong = smooth the sand, new artist, streak restarts. Complete
  when three drawings in a row are guessed. Stay behind the fence line — the cliff is
  real.
- **Where:** Fort Funston, end of Fort Funston Rd off Skyline Blvd; the deck and dunes
  are beside the main lot.
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (the streak restarts until you hit three)
- **Type:** Act it, guess it
- **Photo:** The winning sand drawing with the artist's finger still in it, hang gliders
  (if you're lucky) over the deck behind.
- **Why here:** The only place in SF where a WWII gun battery and a hang glider launch
  share a dune field — and the sand is the biggest sketchpad in the city.

### 2. The Fort Funston Dog Census
- **Do:** Fort Funston is famously SF's off-leash dog paradise. Photograph 8 different
  dogs (from any distance; no petting required — ask the owner first if you want a
  close-up). 8 photos, 8 visibly different dogs = complete.
- **Where:** Fort Funston trails and parking lot — the dogs come to you.
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (persistence — the supply of dogs is effectively infinite on a
  weekend)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** A grid/series of 8 distinct dogs; final shot is the team pointing at their
  favorite.
- **Why here:** Fort Funston is the most famous dog spot in a famously dog-mad city;
  weekend dog density is a genuine local phenomenon.

### 3. The Growing Fish Story
- **Do:** On the public fishing pier just past the Lake Merced Boathouse, tell a fish
  story the memory way: the first teammate says one sentence ("I caught a trout the
  size of a kayak."). The next repeats it and adds a sentence. Around the team it goes,
  each person reciting the whole story so far, word for word, then adding one line.
  Complete when the story reaches TEN sentences with no slips. A slip = the story
  restarts at sentence one.
- **Where:** Lake Merced fishing pier, behind the boathouse at 1 Harding Rd (off Skyline
  Blvd at Harding Park).
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (restart the story)
- **Type:** Memory
- **Photo:** The team on the pier mid-recital, lake and tule reeds behind.
- **Why here:** Lake Merced is the city's only freshwater fishing lake — the natural home
  of the tall tale.

### 4. Anza by Touch
- **Do:** Find the bronze equestrian statue of Juan Bautista de Anza (founder of San
  Francisco, exiled here from Justin Herman Plaza in 2003) on the lake's north shore.
  One teammate closes their eyes and is led to the statue's base. The three parts to find by touch alone are fixed: a stirrup, the sword's hilt, and
  the horse's tail. The blind teammate must lay a hand on each, in any order, with no
  guidance. Three for three
  = complete. A miss = new blind teammate.
- **Where:** Lakeside pullout/parking area off Lake Merced Blvd at the north end of Lake
  Merced — the horse is visible from the road.
- **Time:** 5–10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (new toucher until three for three)
- **Type:** Blind senses
- **Photo:** Eyes shut, hand on the hoof, the founder of San Francisco looming above.
- **Why here:** A full equestrian monument to SF's founder stands weirdly alone in a
  lakeside parking area — and it's the only statue in the game you're allowed to
  explore with your hands.

### 5. Claw Patrol
- **Do:** Descend into Round1, the giant arcade in Stonestown's former Nordstrom
  basement, and win any physical prize from a claw machine. Budget cap: $5 of credits.
  Prize in hand = complete; $5 gone with empty hands = failed.
- **Where:** Round1 Bowling & Arcade, 285 Winston Dr (lower level, Stonestown Galleria).
  Open weekends 10am–2am.
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** ~$5
- **Failable:** retryable (each grab is an attempt; challenge fails when the $5 cap is
  spent)
- **Type:** Aim & throw
- **Photo:** The winner hoisting the prize in front of the wall of claw machines.
- **Why here:** SF's largest arcade opened here in 2024 — Stonestown's second life as
  the west side's indoor playground.

### 6. Ten Stones
- **Do:** On the SFSU campus, find Ruth Asawa's Garden of Remembrance — a waterfall
  garden honoring the 19 Japanese American students sent to internment camps in 1942.
  The large boulders stand one for each WWII camp. Count them, then find the bronze
  scroll marker listing the students' names, and hold up the boulder count on your
  fingers beside it.
- **Where:** SFSU campus, near the J. Paul Leonard Library.
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** The team at the bronze marker, fingers showing the count.
- **Why here:** A quiet, moving memorial by one of SF's greatest artists, hidden on a
  campus most people only drive past.

## Recommended trio

**1, 4, 5** — Play Pictionary in the Fort Funston dunes, find three parts of the Anza
statue by touch alone, and win a claw-machine prize at Round1 on a $5 budget. One
drawing game, one blind-touch game, one skill gamble; one retryable, zero strangers,
~$5, ≈ 40–50 minutes.
