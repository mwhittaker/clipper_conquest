# Western Addition

The Western Addition (official Analysis Neighborhood) is the Fillmore District plus
Cathedral Hill and Anza Vista: the "Harlem of the West," where 1940s–50s Fillmore
Street clubs hosted Billie Holiday and John Coltrane before redevelopment bulldozed
most of it. Today the neighborhood keeps that memory in engraved sidewalk pavers, a
jazz poem etched into blue glass on the Fillmore bridge over the Geary trench, and
murals on Divisadero; it still has the Fillmore Auditorium at Geary & Fillmore and
SF's weirdest-roofed building, St. Mary's Cathedral, on Cathedral Hill. Boundary notes
(checked against the DataSF polygon): Alamo Square is Hayes Valley, everything north
of Geary's centerline (Hamilton Rec, the Boom Boom Room corner, Japan Center) is
Japantown, and the Church of 8 Wheels is Hayes Valley — everything below stays south
of Geary or on Cathedral Hill.

## Candidates

### 1. Sidewalk Time Machine
- **Do:** Walk Fillmore St from Geary Blvd south toward Turk St (check both sidewalks)
  and find THREE different pavers engraved with the names of jazz-era businesses or
  musicians. At the third one, pick a reader: eyes closed, they recite all three names
  in the order you found them, plus one detail from each paver (a street number, a
  word from the description). One slip = walk back, re-read, and start the recital
  over. Complete when a full, error-free recital is on video.
- **Where:** Fillmore St, Geary Blvd south to Turk St (the Fillmore Heritage Center at
  1330 Fillmore & Eddy marks the historic heart).
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (persistence — re-read and restart until it's clean)
- **Type:** Memory
- **Photo:** Three pavers, each with a teammate's shoe beside it, plus the reader
  mid-recital with eyes shut.
- **Why here:** The Fillmore set its lost "Harlem of the West" clubs into the
  pavement; no other SF sidewalk tells this story.

### 2. Marquee Charades
- **Do:** Stand under the Fillmore Auditorium's corner marquee and read off the show
  names posted there and on the building's poster cases (if fewer than five names are
  up, add names from the venue's own upcoming-shows list on your phone until you have
  at least five written down). Number the list. One teammate is the headliner: they generate a random number on a
  phone (re-roll repeats), keep it secret, and act that name out, no words, no letters, no pointing at the sign.
  The team gets ONE guess. Right = the next teammate headlines with a fresh name.
  Wrong = new name, and the streak resets. Complete when the team gets three names in
  a row.
- **Where:** The Fillmore, 1805 Geary Blvd at Fillmore St, southwest corner (exterior
  only).
- **Time:** 5–10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (streak resets until you hit three)
- **Type:** Act it, guess it
- **Photo:** The headliner frozen mid-act with the marquee readable above them, the
  rest of the team mid-shout.
- **Why here:** This 1912 hall, run first by Fillmore legend Charles Sullivan and then
  Bill Graham, is where SF learned its band names; act your way onto the marquee.

### 3. Fourteen Tiers of Silence
- **Do:** Go inside St. Mary's Cathedral (free, open daily until 5 p.m., later on
  Saturdays; if a Mass is underway, play from the back in silence) and stand under the
  altar canopy: a one-ton curtain of thousands of aluminum rods hung on gold wire. Two
  teammates are the counters. On "go," each silently counts how many horizontal tiers
  of rods the canopy has, top to bottom — no talking, no pointing, no fingers until
  the end. When both are done, they hold up their numbers at the same instant. Match =
  complete. Mismatch = both counters walk once around the nave, come back, and
  recount.
- **Where:** Cathedral of St. Mary of the Assumption, 1111 Gough St at Geary Blvd,
  Cathedral Hill (enter from the plaza; visitors welcome outside service times).
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (recount until the two numbers agree)
- **Type:** Mind meld
- **Photo:** The two counters holding up matching numbers, the glittering canopy and
  the four colored window ribbons of the roof cross behind them.
- **Why here:** "Our Lady of Maytag" hides its best trick indoors: a shimmering
  14-tier sculpture that looks countable and, from below, mostly isn't.

### 4. Taboo on the Blue Bridge
- **Do:** Walk out on the Fillmore St bridge over the Geary trench and find the 20
  blue glass panels etched with a jazz poem (an installation called *Three Shades of
  Blue*). Number the panels 1–20 in your head from the Fillmore-Auditorium end. One
  teammate is the describer: they secretly pick a panel, read it, and describe what it
  says in their own words — none of the words on the glass, no numbers, no spelling.
  The team reads along the bridge and points at ONE panel. Right = new describer, new
  panel. Wrong = same describer, new panel, streak resets. Complete at three in a row.
  Tip: the etching is faint; read it with the light behind you, up close.
- **Where:** Fillmore St bridge over Geary Blvd, between the Fillmore (1805 Geary) and
  the north side; stay on the sidewalk side of the panels.
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (new panel until the streak reaches three)
- **Type:** Secret message
- **Photo:** The describer mid-gesture with the team pointing at a blue panel, the
  Geary trench and the 38 buses below.
- **Why here:** A poet laureate's poem about this exact corner, etched in blue glass
  on the bridge that stitches the Fillmore back to Japantown across the expressway
  that split them.

### 5. Mural Memory on Divisadero
- **Do:** Find Mel Waters' 2014 "Harlem of the West" jazz mural on the west side of
  Divisadero between Eddy and Ellis. Everyone studies it for 60 seconds, then the whole
  team turns its back. Three fixed questions, answered as a team: (1) how many
  instruments are painted, (2) how many figures wear a hat, (3) how many figures have
  their eyes closed. Turn around and check by counting together. Three for three =
  complete. Any miss = a new 60 seconds and try again.
- **Where:** 1233 Divisadero St, west side, between Eddy St and Ellis St.
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (new study round until three for three)
- **Type:** Memory
- **Photo:** The team with their backs to the mural mid-answer, then the recount.
- **Why here:** Muralist Mel Waters painted the neighborhood's jazz royalty back onto
  its walls — this is the Divisadero end of the Harlem of the West, and the wall is
  dense enough to punish a lazy memory.

### 6. Call the 38
- **Do:** Climb the Webster St pedestrian bridge over the Geary Blvd trench. Before
  each bus appears, the team calls TWO things out loud on video: its direction
  (DOWNTOWN or OCEAN) and its line (38 local or 38R Rapid — the sign on the front
  shows which). The next 38-family bus to pass under the bridge decides it. Both parts
  right = complete. Wrong = make a new call for the next bus and keep going.
- **Where:** Webster St pedestrian bridge over the Geary expressway trench, Geary Blvd
  & Webster St (the last of the trench's footbridges; the Steiner one came down in
  2020).
- **Time:** 5–15 min (Muni's busiest line; buses come constantly) | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (one call per bus — keep calling until you hit)
- **Type:** Call your shot
- **Photo:** The team on the bridge celebrating, the verdict bus below with its sign
  readable.
- **Why here:** The bridge is a relic of the Geary expressway that reshaped the
  Western Addition — and calling the 38 over its trench is peak transit-race tension.

## Recommended trio

**2, 3, 4** — Act show names off the Fillmore marquee, count the cathedral's
shimmering tiers in silence until two counters agree, and play Taboo with a jazz poem
etched on the blue bridge over the Geary trench. Charades, an agreement count, a
describe-and-find; two at the Fillmore & Geary corner and one on Cathedral Hill (a
10-minute walk east), nothing failable, zero strangers, all $0, ≈ 30–40 minutes.
(Sidewalk Time Machine and Call the 38 are the ready alternates if the bridge panels
prove too faint to read.)
