# Seacliff

Seacliff is one of the smallest of the 41 official neighborhoods: a 1913 master-planned
"residence park" (landscape architect Mark Daniels) of pastel stucco mansions — Willis
Polk and Julia Morgan both built here — draped over the bluffs between the Presidio and
Lincoln Park. Its treasure is China Beach, a pocket cove named for the Chinese fishermen
who anchored their junks and camped there from Gold Rush times (mid-century maps called
it Phelan Beach before the old name won out). Everything below is outdoors, free, and
inside the official boundary — Baker Beach belongs to the Presidio and Lands End to
Lincoln Park, so we stay off both. Note: swimming at China Beach is unsafe and never
part of any challenge — sand only.

## Candidates

### 1. Junk Boat Shipyard
- **Do:** On the sand at China Beach, build a model junk (Chinese sailing boat) at least
  12 inches long from found beach materials only (driftwood, kelp, stones, feathers —
  nothing brought with you). It must have a hull and at least one standing "sail."
  Complete when the finished boat stands on its own on the sand.
- **Where:** China Beach, down the stairway/drive at the seaward end of Sea Cliff Avenue
- **Time:** ~15–20 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (build until it stands)
- **Type:** Build & make
- **Photo:** The finished junk on the sand with the Golden Gate Bridge in the background
- **Why here:** This cove is named for the Chinese fishermen whose junks anchored right
  here — you're relaunching the fleet.

### 2. The Fishermen's Stone, From Memory
- **Do:** Find the 1981 memorial stone at the top of the China Beach access drive
  honoring the Chinese fishermen who camped in the cove since Gold Rush times. One
  teammate, the scribe, studies the four Chinese characters on it for 30 seconds — no
  photos — then walks twenty paces away, turns their back, and draws all four from
  memory on paper or a phone. The team holds the drawing up to the stone: all four
  recognizably right, every stroke group present = complete. Anything missing = another
  30-second look, new scribe.
- **Where:** Seaward end of Sea Cliff Avenue, beside the China Beach entrance/parking
- **Time:** ~10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (look again and redraw)
- **Type:** Memory
- **Photo:** The drawing held up beside the carved characters on the stone.
- **Why here:** The stone is the neighborhood's origin story in granite — the only
  monument in Seacliff, and the hardest four characters your memory will meet today.

### 3. How Far to the Tower?
- **Do:** At the little seaward overlook with the pair of backless memorial benches on
  Sea Cliff Avenue, the whole team looks at the Golden Gate Bridge's south tower and
  agrees on ONE guess: how far away is it, in miles, as the crow flies? Write it down,
  then check with the phone map's distance-measuring tool. Within 20% = complete. Off?
  Guess the distance to Point Bonita Lighthouse across the strait and check that.
- **Where:** North-side overlook on Sea Cliff Avenue, about a block east of the China
  Beach gate (near 29th Ave)
- **Time:** ~5–10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (second target if the first misses)
- **Type:** Guess, then measure
- **Photo:** The guess held up with the bridge behind, map check beside it.
- **Why here:** Seacliff's street-end bridge views are its calling card — and nobody
  guesses the distance right the first time.

### 4. The Only Avenue With a Compass
- **Do:** Phones away, no maps. Somewhere within three blocks of the China Beach gate is
  the only numbered avenue in San Francisco whose name carries a compass direction. Find
  its street sign by walking and reading signs — no asking. Complete when the whole team
  is under the sign and someone reads it aloud on video.
- **Where:** Sea Cliff, within three blocks of Sea Cliff Ave & 28th Ave (the answer is
  on the map; the challenge is finding it without one)
- **Time:** ~10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (keep walking)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** The team under the sign that gives it away.
- **Why here:** A one-of-a-kind street name inside a 1913 gated residence park —
  geography trivia you can only solve on foot.

### 5. Swiss Book Diplomacy
- **Do:** Several consuls general live in Seacliff, and one of them lends books:
  find the pole-mounted Little Free Library kept by the Consulate General of
  Switzerland on Sea Cliff Avenue just uphill from the China Beach entrance. Borrow
  (or donate) one book. For full credit, also find one OTHER Little Free Library in
  the neighborhood (there are more along El Camino del Mar near 29th/30th Ave). Two
  different libraries visited + one book in hand = complete.
- **Where:** Sea Cliff Ave by the China Beach gate; second library along El Camino
  del Mar around 29th–30th Ave
- **Time:** ~10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (keep hunting)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** A teammate holding the borrowed book at the open door of the Swiss
  consulate's library
- **Why here:** Only in Seacliff is the neighborhood lending library run by a foreign
  consulate.

### 6. The Beach Wager
- **Do:** At the top of the China Beach stairway, before anyone can see the sand, every
  teammate secretly writes down a guess: how many people will be on the beach when you
  reach the bottom? Then walk down together and count everyone on the sand (not in the
  water, not on the stairs). If at least one teammate's guess is within 3 of the count,
  you win. One attempt only — no peeking over the rail before guesses are locked.
- **Where:** China Beach stairway, from the parking area at the end of Sea Cliff Avenue
  down to the beach
- **Time:** ~10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** one-shot (guesses lock before the first step)
- **Type:** Guess, then measure
- **Photo:** The winning guess held up on the sand, the cove behind.
- **Why here:** The long stair-and-ramp plunge to the cove is the neighborhood's
  signature descent — and the beach below is never the same size twice.

## Recommended trio

**1, 2, 4** — Build a beach-found junk that stands on its own at China Beach, redraw
the four characters of the Fishermen's Stone from memory, and find the only numbered
avenue in San Francisco with a compass direction — on foot, no map. A build, a memory
game, a deduction; nothing failable, zero strangers, all $0, ≈ 35–45 minutes. (The
China Beach stairway wager stays the alternate one-shot.)
