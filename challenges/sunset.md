# Sunset/Parkside

SF's foggiest quarter: a giant pastel grid of Doelger-built rowhouses running downhill
into the longest stretch of Ocean Beach, with beloved food strips on Irving, Noriega, and
Taraval, the brand-new car-free Sunset Dunes park on the old Great Highway, and Stern
Grove's hidden eucalyptus canyon at the southern edge. (Boundary check against the
official Analysis Neighborhoods map: the 16th Avenue Tiled Steps and Grandview Park fall
in Inner Sunset, and the Doggie Diner head falls in Lakeshore — so none of those are used
here.)

## Candidates

### 1. Bigger Than Your Biggest Teammate
- **Do:** On the sand at Ocean Beach (between Lincoln Way and Sloat Blvd), write your
  team name — or team color, if the name is long — in letters drawn in the sand, each
  letter TALLER than your tallest teammate. To prove scale, that teammate lies down in
  the sand next to one letter. Complete when the photo shows every letter plus the
  human yardstick.
- **Where:** Ocean Beach, easiest access from the Judah, Noriega, or Taraval stairwells
  off the old Great Highway
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (smooth the sand and redraw until it's right)
- **Type:** Build & make
- **Photo:** Shot from the seawall/dune above: giant sand letters with a teammate lying
  beside one for scale, ocean behind
- **Why here:** This is SF's longest, widest run of open beach — the only neighborhood
  with enough sand for person-sized typography.

### 2. Spin the Wheel of Fortune at Polly Ann
- **Do:** At Polly Ann Ice Cream (scooping on Noriega since 1955), ask the counter to
  spin their famous flavor Wheel of Fortune. One player buys a single scoop of whatever
  flavor the wheel lands on — durian included — and takes at least three bites.
  Complete when the wheel's flavor is bought and bitten.
- **Where:** Polly Ann Ice Cream, 3142 Noriega St at 39th Ave (open daily from late
  morning)
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** ~$6
- **Failable:** no (the wheel decides the flavor, but eating it is always possible —
  that's the fun)
- **Type:** Pure luck
- **Photo:** The spinner mid-lick of the mystery flavor, pointing at the wheel behind
  the counter
- **Why here:** Polly Ann's spin-the-wheel-and-eat-what-you-get ritual is a one-of-a-kind
  Sunset institution — roulette you can taste.

### 3. The Doelger Rainbow
- **Do:** The Sunset is the world's biggest showcase of identical pastel stucco
  rowhouses. Find one block face where FIVE consecutive attached houses are each painted
  a clearly different color, and line your whole team up in front of them, one player
  per house. Complete when the photo shows five adjacent, five-colored facades.
- **Where:** Anywhere in the avenues — the blocks between Irving and Vicente from
  ~30th to 47th Ave are prime hunting ground
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (keep walking blocks until you find a run of five)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** Five teammates (or fewer, spread out) each fronting a different-colored
  house in an unbroken pastel row
- **Why here:** Henry Doelger's "white cliffs" of the Sunset are the neighborhood's
  defining look — this turns the famous sameness into a color scavenger hunt.

### 4. Roadhouse in the Woods
- **Do:** Enter Stern Grove and descend into the eucalyptus canyon to find the
  Trocadero Clubhouse, the white-and-green 1892 Victorian roadhouse hiding at the
  bottom. Complete when your whole team is standing on its front porch/steps.
- **Where:** Stern Grove — enter at 19th Ave & Sloat Blvd and follow the main path down;
  the Trocadero is near the west end of the concert meadow
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Go there
- **Photo:** Full team posed like an old-timey band on the gingerbread porch of the
  Victorian
- **Why here:** A gold-rush-era roadhouse at the bottom of a fog-forest canyon in the
  middle of the avenues — most San Franciscans don't know it exists.

### 5. The Plover Stand
- **Do:** Walk out onto Sunset Dunes (the car-free former Great Highway) and find any
  posted Snowy Plover protection sign along the dune fencing. Then every teammate holds
  a one-legged "plover stand" next to it for a fully counted 30 seconds. If anyone's
  second foot touches down, the attempt fails; you get 3 attempts total.
- **Where:** Sunset Dunes park, between Lincoln Way and Sloat Blvd (dune-fence signs
  are posted along the beach side)
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** retryable (an attempt = one 30-second team hold; max 3 attempts)
- **Type:** Balance & carry
- **Photo:** The whole team frozen on one leg beside the plover sign, dunes and ocean
  behind
- **Why here:** Ocean Beach's dunes are the protected wintering ground of the snowy
  plover — the tiny shorebird this stretch of coast is famous for guarding (and that a
  local coffee drink is named after).

### 6. Beignet o'Clock on Noriega
- **Do:** Join the line at Devil's Teeth Baking Company and buy one of their
  weekend-only beignets (any pastry under $5 counts if beignets are sold out). Complete
  when the purchase is in hand outside the shop.
- **Where:** Devil's Teeth Baking Company, 3876 Noriega St near 45th Ave (weekends
  roughly 7am–3pm; beignets go until they sell out — a late-afternoon game breaks this
  one, so schedule accordingly)
- **Time:** 10–20 min (the line is part of the legend) | **Cost:** ~$3–5
- **Failable:** no (the fallback pastry keeps it completable any time the shop is open)
- **Type:** Eat & buy
- **Photo:** Powdered-sugar-dusted grin holding the beignet in front of the teal
  storefront
- **Why here:** Devil's Teeth is the Outer Sunset's surf-town breakfast institution;
  its weekend beignet line is a neighborhood ritual.

## Recommended trio

**1, 2, 3** — Beach, food strip, and the pastel avenues: three distinct faces of the
Sunset within about 10 blocks of each other (Ocean Beach → Noriega & 39th → any nearby
avenue block). Vibe spread is make (giant sand letters), taste-with-a-spin-of-luck
(Polly Ann's wheel), and find/count (the Doelger rainbow); nothing failable, nothing
stranger-dependent, one cheap purchase that IS the fun, and roughly 30–40 minutes of
challenge time total.
