# Marina

The Marina is 1915 World's Fair land: the Palace of Fine Arts is the fair's last
ghost, the flat blocks were built on its rubble (which is why the 1989 quake hit
here hardest, and why a fireboat named Phoenix is a local hero), and the
waterfront is all kites, masts, and Golden Gate views from Fort Mason to the
yacht harbor. Boundary note, verified: Crissy Field sits inside the Presidio's
federal boundary and belongs to the **Presidio** Analysis Neighborhood, not this
one — everything below stays east of Lyon Street. Anchors: the Palace, the Wave
Organ jetty, Marina Green, Fort Mason's Great Meadow, and Chestnut Street.

## Candidates

### 1. Masterpiece, One Guess
- **Do:** The Palace of Fine Arts is an art thing — so make art. One teammate, the
  artist, secretly picks a famous painting (one they believe the whole team has seen).
  Anywhere on the Palace grounds, the artist recreates it by any means: drawn with
  bought supplies (up to $10), scratched in dirt, arranged from leaves and sticks, or
  built as a living tableau with the colonnade as backdrop. When the artist says "done,"
  the rest of the team gets ONE collective guess at the painting's name. Right =
  complete. Wrong = failed. One shot.
- **Where:** Palace of Fine Arts grounds, 3601 Lyon St — lagoon lawn, colonnade, or
  rotunda.
- **Time:** 15–20 min | **Cost:** $0–10
- **Failable:** one-shot
- **Type:** Act it, guess it
- **Photo:** The recreation side by side with the artist's reveal of the real painting
  on their phone, rotunda behind.
- **Why here:** A palace built for fine art deserves at least one Mona Lisa made of
  eucalyptus leaves.

### 2. Play the Bay's Pipe Organ
- **Do:** Walk the jetty at the end of Yacht Rd (past the St. Francis and Golden Gate
  yacht clubs) to the Wave Organ, the 1986 acoustic sculpture of 25 pipes played by the
  tides. Record a video that clearly captures the organ's sound — the gurgles, slaps
  and hums of the water in the pipes — with a teammate's ear at the pipe in frame. Too
  faint to hear on playback? Try another pipe, or wait: the organ is loudest near high
  tide.
- **Where:** Wave Organ, end of the yacht-harbor jetty, Yacht Rd off Marina Blvd
  (open-air, 24/7).
- **Time:** 20 min incl. the jetty walk | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (keep trying pipes, or wait for the tide)
- **Type:** Go there
- **Photo:** The video is the proof — the bay's own music, with your team listening in.
- **Why here:** A wave-powered organ built out of cemetery headstones at the end of a
  yacht-harbor jetty exists exactly once on Earth, and it's here.

### 3. Bridge, Rock, and Boat
- **Do:** From anywhere along the Marina Green waterfront, capture ONE photo
  that contains all three: the Golden Gate Bridge, Alcatraz Island, and a
  sailboat with its sail up. All three must be identifiable in a single frame
  (no stitching).
- **Where:** Marina Green promenade, Marina Blvd between Scott and Webster.
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** retryable (each submitted frame is an attempt; keep shooting
  until a sailboat crosses your composition)
- **Type:** Frame the shot
- **Photo:** The trophy shot itself — bridge, prison island, and sail in one
  frame.
- **Why here:** Marina Green is the only lawn in the city where those three
  line up across open water; on a weekend day the bay is full of sails.

### 4. Filibuster at Fort Mason
- **Do:** Climb to the Great Meadow at Fort Mason and find the huge bronze of
  Congressman Phillip Burton caught mid-speech. One teammate matches his pose
  exactly — right arm thrust out, left hand at the chest, mouth open like
  you're shouting a national park into existence — while standing beside him.
  Complete when the photo shows statue and human in matching poses.
- **Where:** Great Meadow, upper Fort Mason (enter from Bay St & Franklin or
  the stairs from Marina Blvd); open-air, 24/7.
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Performance
- **Photo:** Side-by-side oratory: bronze Burton and your loudest teammate,
  both in full and fiery flight.
- **Why here:** Burton wrote the law that created the Golden Gate National
  Recreation Area, so his statue yells forever over the parkland he made —
  starting with this meadow.

### 5. Weep With the Ladies
- **Do:** At the Palace of Fine Arts colonnade, find the giant "weeping women" sculpted
  atop the planter boxes — backs turned, heads bowed on their arms. Every teammate
  lines up and holds the exact pose directly below a row of them.
- **Where:** Palace of Fine Arts colonnade, 3601 Lyon St.
- **Time:** 5–10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Performance
- **Photo:** The whole team in the pose with at least one stone weeping woman above.
- **Why here:** The Palace's most famous residents, mourning for a century.

### 6. Lagoon Bird Bingo
- **Do:** At the Palace of Fine Arts lagoon, photograph THREE different kinds
  of birds (e.g., duck, gull, pigeon, coot, egret, heron, swan — any three
  distinct species, one clear photo each, all taken this visit). Bonus flavor:
  the trees here are a known roost for black-crowned night herons, hunched
  grumpy birds hiding in plain sight.
- **Where:** Palace of Fine Arts lagoon path, Baker St side.
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (ducks, gulls, and pigeons guarantee it with persistence;
  a heron makes it bragging rights)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** A three-shot collage of your birds, lagoon and rotunda behind.
- **Why here:** The fair's ornamental lagoon became an accidental bird
  sanctuary; sharp eyes can win this one single-handed.

## Recommended trio

**1, 2, 3** — Recreate a famous painting on the Palace of Fine Arts grounds and give
your team one guess (the neighborhood's one-shot), record the Wave Organ actually
playing, and land the Golden Gate + Alcatraz + sailboat in a single
frame. An art game, a sound recording, a patience shot; one one-shot, one retryable, zero
strangers, $0–10, ≈ 40–50 minutes.
