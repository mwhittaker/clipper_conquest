# Chinatown

The oldest Chinatown in North America: ~24 dense blocks bounded by Bush, Broadway,
Kearny, and Powell, entered through the Dragon Gate and threaded with hidden alleys
(Ross, Spofford, Waverly "Street of Painted Balconies"). Anchors here are the Grant
Avenue dragon streetlamps (installed 1925), the hand-folded cookies of the Golden Gate
Fortune Cookie Factory, the Stockton Street produce markets, and the mythology-themed
Willie "Woo Woo" Wong Playground. Note: Portsmouth Square closed in June 2026 for a
~2-year, $73M renovation — nothing below depends on it.

## Candidates

### 1. The Ransom-Note Fortune
- **Do:** Buy one bag at the Golden Gate Fortune Cookie Factory. Crack a cookie, tear
  the fortune into scraps carrying one letter each, and start assembling the phrase
  **SAN FRAN** ransom-note style (S, A, A, N, N, F, R — the F is the hard part; plenty
  of fortunes don't have one). Only letters torn from fortune slips count, each scrap
  used once. Keep cracking cookies from your bag until you can lay out the full
  phrase. If an entire bag can't produce it, buy another and reflect on your luck.
- **Where:** Golden Gate Fortune Cookie Factory, 56 Ross Alley (between Washington &
  Jackson); open daily, until ~7pm weekends
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** ~$5–8 for the bag (a small photo fee may apply inside)
- **Failable:** no (persistence — every cracked cookie is progress, and a snack)
- **Type:** Word game
- **Photo:** SAN FRAN assembled from torn destiny on a step in Ross Alley, the pile of
  sacrificed cookies alongside.
- **Why here:** Hand-folded fortunes since 1962 in a gambling-den-era alley — and yes,
  locals wince at "San Fran," but spelling it out of your own fortunes absolves you.

### 2. The Lamplighter's Census
- **Do:** Grant Avenue's cast-iron dragon streetlamps have coiled on their posts since
  1925. Stand at the Dragon Gate (Bush & Grant). BEFORE anyone takes a step up Grant,
  the team locks in ONE written estimate: how many dragon lampposts stand on Grant
  between the gate and Broadway? No research, no peeking up the street beyond what you
  can see from the gate. Then walk all eight blocks and count every lamp as a team.
  Estimate within ±15% of your count = win. You get exactly one estimate, ever.
- **Where:** Grant Ave from the Dragon Gate (at Bush) to Broadway
- **Time:** 15–20 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** one-shot
- **Type:** Guess, then measure
- **Photo:** The written estimate held up at the Dragon Gate before the walk, then the
  final tally beside the last lamp at Broadway.
- **Why here:** Chinatown's mascot hides in plain sight on every block — betting on
  exactly how many is the neighborhood's own counting-house game.

### 3. The Hand Knows
- **Do:** At any Stockton Street produce market, the buyer picks THREE different
  fruits — any fruits at all, from the everyday to the unpronounceable — hidden from
  one blindfolded teammate. The guesser holds each fruit for up to 30 seconds — touch only,
  no smelling, no tasting, no hints — and names it. Two of three correct = complete.
  Fewer = retry with three new fruits and a new guesser. Then eat your props.
- **Where:** Stockton St between Sacramento St and Broadway (market blocks)
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** ~$3–6 per attempt
- **Failable:** retryable (three new fruits per attempt)
- **Type:** Blind senses
- **Photo:** The blindfolded guesser gingerly palming a dragonfruit, market bins
  spilling behind — then the whole team mid-bite.
- **Why here:** Stockton's sidewalk bins stack fruit most visitors can't name with
  their eyes open — naming them blind is the market's black-belt exam.

### 4. The Painted Balconies of Waverly Place
- **Do:** Walk the two blocks of Waverly Place, the "Street of Painted Balconies," and
  photograph wrought-iron or wooden balconies painted in at least 4 different colors
  (e.g., red, green, yellow, teal). Complete when you have 4 distinct balcony colors,
  each clearly on a different balcony.
- **Where:** Waverly Place, between Sacramento St and Washington St (one block west of
  Grant)
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** A teammate pointing up at the 4th color's balcony (keep the other three
  shots in the upload).
- **Why here:** Waverly's stacked, rainbow-painted temple and association balconies are
  the neighborhood's most photographed hidden street — the colors ARE the landmark.

### 5. Year of the Hunt
- **Do:** Find and photograph EIGHT of the twelve Chinese zodiac animals anywhere in
  the neighborhood — statues, murals, signs, shop goods, restaurant windows all count.
  One rule: no two animals may come from the same thing (a zodiac wheel or placemat
  showing many animals counts for only one of your eight). Complete at eight.
- **Where:** Anywhere in the neighborhood; Grant Ave shops and restaurant windows are
  rich hunting grounds
- **Time:** 15–20 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** Each find pointed at; the keeper is a teammate beside their own birth-year
  animal.
- **Why here:** The zodiac is woven through every block of the oldest Chinatown in
  North America — and suddenly everyone remembers exactly what year they were born.

### 6. Ride the Water Dragon
- **Do:** At Willie "Woo Woo" Wong Playground, find the giant carved water dragon that
  wraps around the two-level play tower. Every teammate goes down the tower slide once, and the team locates the phoenix sculpture
  bridging the tot zone. Complete when everyone has slid and the phoenix is found.
- **Where:** 830 Sacramento St, between Waverly Place and Pagoda Place
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Body
- **Photo:** A teammate mid-slide with the dragon's carved body in frame.
- **Why here:** A $14.5M playground themed on Chinese mythology, named for Chinatown's
  own 5'5" USF basketball legend — the neighborhood's most joyful corner.

## Recommended trio

**1, 2, 3** — Three games on the classic south-to-north spine: commit your lamppost
census at the Dragon Gate, spell SAN FRAN from torn-up fortunes in Ross Alley, and
read fruit by touch on Stockton. Wordplay, a one-shot estimate, and a blind-touch
test — the neighborhood's one one-shot gamble is here, balanced by two retryables; no
strangers, everything weekend-reliable, ~$10–14 total, ≈ 35–50 minutes on foot.
