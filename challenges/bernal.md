# Bernal Heights

Bernal is a grassy dome of a neighborhood: a wild 360°-view summit ringed by a paved
loop, community-built stairways and slides carved into its slopes, and the small-town
main street of Cortland Avenue at its southern foot. The official analysis neighborhood
runs from Cesar Chavez (north) to the I-280/Alemany interchange (south), San Jose Ave
(west) to US-101 (east) — so Precita Park, Holly Park, and the Alemany markets are all
in-bounds, while Balmy Alley and Mitchell's Ice Cream are not. Everything below anchors
on the hill itself and the odd, community-made things Bernal bolted onto it.

## Candidates

### 1. Cardboard Curling at the Twin Slides
- **Do:** At Esmeralda Slide Park, every teammate rides the twin 40-foot slides at least
  once (grab a piece of the cardboard traditionally left at the top, or bring your own).
  Then the game: mark a target zone at the bottom of one slide — a shoe placed one
  stride past the slide's lip. Send an EMPTY piece of cardboard down the slide, no rider,
  no push after release. Complete when a piece comes to rest touching the shoe.
  Overshoots and stalls are retries; rotate throwers; clear the run-out between goes.
- **Where:** Esmeralda Slide Park, on the Esmeralda Ave stairway between Winfield St and
  Prospect Ave (community street park, open daylight hours, free).
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (keep sliding cardboard)
- **Type:** Aim & throw
- **Photo:** The cardboard kissing the shoe at the bottom of the slide, the team leaning
  in like a curling rink.
- **Why here:** These are Bernal's legendary neighbor-built slides — Mayor Dianne
  Feinstein raced a supervisor down them at the 1979 opening; curling is the next
  sport they were built for.

### 2. I Spy From Sutrito
- **Do:** Climb to the summit of Bernal Hill (the little radio tower locals call
  "Sutrito," a mini Sutro Tower) and play I Spy with the whole city as the board. The
  spy picks anything visible from the top — a tower, a bridge, a hill, a boat, a water
  tank — and gives the classic clue ("I spy something red and pointy"). Teammates guess
  by pointing — THREE guesses each per round; the spy says yes or no. Found = the next
  teammate is the spy. Nobody finds it = the spy reveals, and takes another turn with a
  new object.
  Complete when every teammate has been the spy once and had their thing found.
- **Where:** Bernal Heights Park summit, off Bernal Heights Blvd (open 24/7; the
  Esmeralda stairway from the slides leads straight up).
- **Time:** 15–20 min including the climb | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** The team at the summit fence, everyone pointing at the last thing spied,
  360° of city behind.
- **Why here:** Bernal Hill's 466-ft bald summit is one of the only true 360° panoramas
  in SF — the biggest I Spy board in the city.

### 3. Doves & Melted Guns
- **Do:** In Precita Park, find the peace memorial: a bench made of cedar and metal from
  melted-down guns, resting on a dove-shaped platform of ~900 tiles hand-painted by
  neighbors in the 1990s. Sit the whole team on the bench, then find and photograph 3
  different painted tiles: one with a heart, one with a sun or star, and one with an
  animal (any 3 clearly different pictures count if those exact motifs have worn away).
- **Where:** Precita Park, Precita Ave & Folsom St, on the side across from Precita Eyes
  (348 Precita Ave), Bernal's famous mural studio.
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** Team on the melted-gun bench with the dove platform visible, plus close-ups
  of the 3 tiles.
- **Why here:** Guns literally turned into a park bench by Bernal neighbors, on the
  block where the Precita Eyes mural movement was born — it exists nowhere else.

### 4. One Foot on the Steepest Street
- **Do:** Walk to the top block of Bradford Street above Tompkins Avenue — at a 41%
  grade, the steepest street in San Francisco (Lombard's crooked block is only 27%). On
  the sidewalk, every teammate stands on ONE foot, facing downhill, for a fully counted
  20 seconds at the same time. Anyone's second foot touches down = everyone restarts the
  count. Complete on one clean 20.
- **Where:** Bradford St between Tompkins Ave and Jarboe Ave, east slope of Bernal Hill.
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (restart the count)
- **Type:** Balance & carry
- **Photo:** The flamingo line on the 41% sidewalk, street sign in frame.
- **Why here:** Tourists queue at Lombard and Filbert; Bernal quietly holds the city's
  actual steepest street, and it is genuinely hard to stand on.

### 5. Treasure Run at Alemany
- **Do:** Go to 100 Alemany Blvd, home of the Alemany Farmers' Market — founded 1943,
  California's first farmers' market (Saturdays 7am–2pm) — which flips into the Alemany
  Flea Market on Sundays (7am–3pm). Saturday: buy one fruit or vegetable for $5 or less
  and every teammate takes a bite. Sunday: buy one treasure for $5 or less that is older
  than the oldest teammate (a printed date or maker's mark proves it). Purchase made + photo taken = complete.
- **Where:** Alemany Farmers' Market / Flea Market, 100 Alemany Blvd (at the south tip
  of the neighborhood; note the Saturday market closes at 2pm).
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** ~$5
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Eat & buy
- **Photo:** The team holding the purchase at the vendor's stall — mid-bite on Saturday,
  treasure aloft on Sunday.
- **Why here:** The oldest farmers' market in California lives at Bernal's southern
  edge, and its weekend double identity (produce/flea) is a neighborhood institution.

### 6. Pace the Perfect Circle
- **Do:** Holly Park sits inside Holly Circle — a street that is a complete circle, one
  of SF's geometric oddities, ringing one of the city's oldest parks. At the Holly Park
  sign, the team agrees on ONE guess: how many of your tallest teammate's paces is one
  full lap of the park's perimeter path? Write it down, then that teammate walks the lap
  at a normal stride, everyone counting out loud. Within 10% = complete. Off? One more
  guess — the inner loop — and pace that. Miss twice = failed.
- **Where:** Holly Park, ringed by Holly Circle (between Highland Ave, Park St, and
  Murray St).
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** retryable (two loops, two guesses)
- **Type:** Guess, then measure
- **Photo:** The guess and the real count side by side at the sign, Bernal Hill behind.
- **Why here:** A park whose address is a perfect circle is pure Bernal geography — and
  a circle is the hardest shape to guess the length of.

## Recommended trio

**1, 2, 4** — Cardboard curling on the Esmeralda slides, I Spy with the whole city
from the summit of Bernal Hill, and a one-footed 20 seconds on the steepest sidewalk in
San Francisco. One aiming game, one guessing game, one balance test; nothing failable,
zero strangers, all $0, ≈ 35–45 minutes.
