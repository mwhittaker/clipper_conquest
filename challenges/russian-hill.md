# Russian Hill

Russian Hill (roughly Van Ness to Columbus, Broadway/Pacific up to Bay Street) is the
city's vertical playground: the eight-switchback "crookedest" block of Lombard, the
31.5% Filbert Street wall — among the steepest street blocks in SF — and a secret
network of stairway gardens like Macondray Lane and Ina Coolbrith Park, all threaded by
the Powell–Hyde cable car. I anchored on the hill's three superpowers: absurd slopes,
hidden pedestrian lanes, and postcard views — plus the 1948 birthplace of Swensen's.

## Candidates

### 1. Walk the Crookedest Street
- **Do:** Walk the famous crooked block of Lombard Street from top (Hyde) to bottom
  (Leavenworth) on the sidewalk stairs. That's it — the street is the challenge.
- **Where:** Lombard St, 1000 block, between Hyde St and Leavenworth St.
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Go there
- **Photo:** The team at the bottom with the brick switchbacks and hydrangeas rising
  behind.
- **Why here:** The "crookedest street in the world" is Russian Hill's global calling
  card.

### 2. Stand Up Straight on the 31.5%
- **Do:** Filbert Street between Leavenworth and Hyde is one of the steepest blocks in
  San Francisco. On a SLOPED part of the sidewalk (not a flat stair tread), build
  something at least ONE FOOT tall that stands truly upright — plumb to gravity, not
  tilted with the hill. Use anything you're carrying, find, or buy nearby (up to $10).
  It must stand on its own for a count of ten with no hands touching it, and a phone
  level app held against it must read within 5° of vertical. Falls or leans too far?
  Rebuild and try again.
- **Where:** Filbert St between Leavenworth St and Hyde St (sidewalk only; keep
  everything off the roadway). Shops for supplies along Hyde St a block or two south
  and on Polk St a few blocks west.
- **Time:** 15–25 min | **Cost:** up to ~$10 (optional)
- **Failable:** no (rebuild until it stands)
- **Type:** Build & make
- **Photo:** The tower standing dead vertical against the plunging street, level app
  reading shown, team hands in the air.
- **Why here:** On a 31.5% grade "straight up" is the hard part — everything wants to
  lean with the hill, and you have to out-engineer it.

### 3. Find Barbary Lane
- **Do:** Locate the hidden pedestrian lane that inspired "28 Barbary Lane" in Tales of
  the City and traverse it end to end: enter at Jones & Union area (Macondray Lane
  runs between Green and Union) and walk east until you descend the wooden plank
  stairway that drops you out onto Taylor Street. Everyone must touch the Taylor Street
  sidewalk at the bottom.
- **Where:** Macondray Lane, between Jones St and Taylor St (parallel to and between
  Green and Union); the wooden stairs are the Taylor St end.
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** Team on the rickety-charming wooden stairway under the tree canopy, with
  a "Macondray Lane" street sign or the Taylor St exit in frame.
- **Why here:** A cobbled, gardens-and-cottages footpath with no roadway and a secret
  wooden staircase — a National Register historic district most visitors never find.

### 4. Couplet for the Poet
- **Do:** Climb the stair-garden of Ina Coolbrith Park (named for California's first
  poet laureate) to an upper terrace with a Bay view. Pick a poet. The rest of the team
  hands the poet ONE word to end the first line with; the poet must immediately write a
  two-line poem about the other team whose second line ends in a rhyme for that word.
  Real rhyme = complete. No rhyme within a minute = the team picks a new word and the
  poet tries again.
- **Where:** Ina Coolbrith Park, Vallejo St at Taylor St (stairs continue up the Vallejo
  Street steps).
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (new word until it rhymes)
- **Type:** Word game
- **Photo:** The poet holding the handwritten couplet, Bay Bridge panorama behind.
- **Why here:** A hidden hillside park honoring SF's bohemian poet laureate — the only
  place in the city where writing bad poetry is basically mandatory.

### 5. Postcard Ambush
- **Do:** Recreate SF's most famous postcard, live: ONE photo containing a moving
  Powell–Hyde cable car, the Bay with Alcatraz behind it, and at least one teammate.
  Stake out your angle and time the shot; if a car passes and you miss, wait for the
  next one (roughly every 10 minutes on weekends).
- **Where:** Hyde St between Lombard and Bay (Hyde & Greenwich or Hyde & Lombard crests
  give the Alcatraz backdrop).
- **Time:** 5–20 min (depends on cable car timing) | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (another car always comes)
- **Type:** Frame the shot
- **Photo:** The proof IS the challenge: cable car + Alcatraz + teammate in one frame.
- **Why here:** The Hyde Street line cresting Russian Hill with Alcatraz behind is the
  most reproduced image of San Francisco — now with you in it.

### 6. First Scoop on Earth
- **Do:** Buy one scoop (any flavor) at the original Swensen's — the 1948 corner shop
  where the worldwide chain began — and finish it before leaving the corner of Union &
  Hyde. Completion = empty cone/cup shown at the corner.
- **Where:** Swensen's Ice Cream, 1999 Hyde St (at Union St). Note: typically opens
  around noon on weekends — schedule this for afternoon passes.
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** ~$6–8
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Eat & buy
- **Photo:** Teammate mid-lick under the vintage neon SWENSEN'S sign, cable car tracks
  on Hyde in view.
- **Why here:** Earle Swensen opened this exact corner shop in 1948; every Swensen's
  on the planet descends from this one.

## Recommended trio

**1, 5, 2** — Walk down the crookedest block in the world (the trio's "the place is
the game" entry), land the cable car + Alcatraz timing shot on Hyde, and build a
freestanding tower that stands truly vertical on the 31.5% Filbert Street slope. One
icon, one timing shot, one engineering puzzle; nothing failable, zero strangers,
$0–10, ≈ 40–55 minutes.
