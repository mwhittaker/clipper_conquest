# Glen Park

Glen Park is a village hiding inside a big city: a two-block commercial pocket at
Chenery & Diamond (indie bookstore, taqueria, market) sitting directly on top of a BART
station, with a genuinely wild canyon — Glen Canyon Park, free-flowing Islais Creek,
red chert outcrops, the site of America's first dynamite factory — starting three blocks
away. On its Noe Valley edge, the Fairmount hillside stacks up quirky Laidley Street
houses, one of SF's last wooden street-staircases (Harry Street), and the bare rock
summit of Billy Goat Hill. Challenges anchor on those three worlds: canyon, village,
hilltop.

## Candidates

### 1. Kaboom at the Dynamite Diamond
- **Do:** In 1868 Alfred Nobel licensed his brand-new invention to the Giant Powder
  Company, which built America's FIRST dynamite factory in Glen Canyon — and in 1869 it
  blew itself to smithereens. The ballfield sits on the factory site, and a bronze
  plaque (California Historical Landmark No. 1002, on a low concrete slab near the
  park's southern entrance by the rec center and ballfield) marks it. Find the plaque,
  then light the fuse: one teammate starts a stopwatch and hides the screen. Everyone
  else counts in their heads — no watches, no counting out loud — and the team's
  detonator yells "KABOOM!" when the team agrees exactly 60 seconds have passed. Within
  3 seconds of 60 = complete. Off by more = new detonator, new fuse.
- **Where:** Glen Canyon Park southern entrance, by the rec center/ballfield (enter at
  Elk St near Chenery, or Bosworth & O'Shaughnessy)
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (relight the fuse until you nail it)
- **Type:** Guess, then measure
- **Photo:** The stopwatch frozen somewhere between 0:57 and 1:03, held beside the
  dynamite plaque, team mid-"KABOOM."
- **Why here:** The first dynamite factory in the United States exploded on this exact
  spot — a 60-second fuse is the least you can do.

### 2. Leaf Boat Race on Islais Creek
- **Do:** Walk the Gum Tree Girls Trail into the canyon floor to the wooden boardwalk
  where Islais Creek runs through the willows. Each teammate picks a fallen eucalyptus
  ("gum tree") leaf as their boat. Launch them together from the upstream side of the
  boardwalk; complete when any leaf floats ten feet downstream without snagging. Snagged
  fleet = new leaves, new launch. Stay on the boardwalk and the path.
- **Where:** Glen Canyon Park — Gum Tree Girls Trail (starts behind the rec center) to
  the creekside boardwalk
- **Time:** 15–20 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (relaunch until one sails)
- **Type:** Aim & throw
- **Photo:** Leaves mid-voyage, the team cheering from the boardwalk.
- **Why here:** A wild creek canyon in the middle of SF that exists only because three
  neighborhood women beat a freeway — the creek still runs, and it still races.

### 3. Bird & Beckett, Meet Bird & Beckett
- **Do:** The village's beloved bookstore/record shop is named for jazz legend Charlie
  "Bird" Parker and playwright Samuel Beckett. Honor both namesakes: dig through the
  vinyl bins for a Charlie Parker record (any record with a bird pictured on the sleeve
  also counts), and find a book by Samuel Beckett on the shelves. Complete when one
  teammate holds the record and another holds the book in the same photo inside the
  shop or under its sign. Buying a cheap used paperback afterward: optional, classy.
- **Where:** Bird & Beckett Books & Records, 653 Chenery St (open Sat & Sun 12–6 pm;
  closed Mondays)
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0 (optional ~$5 used book)
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** teammates holding the Bird record and the Beckett book side by side at the
  shop
- **Why here:** A bookstore that doubles as a jazz club is Glen Park's living-room —
  and its name is a two-part riddle you solve by crate-digging.

### 4. Telephone Up Harry Street
- **Do:** Harry Street is one of the last wooden street-staircases in San Francisco —
  roughly 240 steps climbing a hidden green tunnel between houses, marked only by tiny
  "Harry" street signs at each end. Play telephone up it: the team spreads out along
  the stairs, at least ten steps between each person. The bottom teammate writes down a
  made-up sentence of at least eight words, then whispers it once to the next person
  up; they whisper it once to the next; and so on to the top. The top teammate says it
  out loud and the bottom one reveals the paper. Word-for-word match = complete.
  Anything garbled = new sentence, and everyone shuffles up one position. Then finish
  the climb to the rocky summit of Billy Goat Hill above Beacon St.
- **Where:** Harry Street steps (Laidley St near Harper St, up to Beacon St), then
  Billy Goat Hill summit
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (new sentence until one survives the climb)
- **Type:** Secret message
- **Photo:** The team strung up the wooden stairs mid-whisper, then the paper held up
  at Billy Goat Hill with the skyline behind.
- **Why here:** A secret wooden staircase in a green tunnel — quiet enough for a whisper
  to travel 240 steps, if your team is any good.

### 5. The Baby Burrito Handoff
- **Do:** La Corneta has anchored the village for 30+ years, and its cult item is the
  "baby burrito" — everything a super burrito has, shrunk. Buy exactly one baby
  burrito. Before anyone bites, photograph it lying across one teammate's open palm for
  scale. Then the team must finish it with every teammate taking at least one bite.
- **Where:** La Corneta Taqueria, 2834 Diamond St (Sat 10 am–10 pm, Sun 11 am–9 pm)
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** ~$6
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Eat & buy
- **Photo:** the palm-sized baby burrito resting on an outstretched hand, hungry team
  crowded behind it
- **Why here:** Splitting one tiny burrito six ways at Glen Park's 30-year taqueria
  institution is peak village lunch.

### 6. Recite the Line at Glen Park BART
- **Do:** Glen Park's 1973 BART station — raw board-formed concrete and rusting Corten
  steel — is routinely named one of the finest Brutalist transit buildings in America.
  Inside, find the system map. One teammate studies it for 60 seconds, then turns away
  and recites every station from Glen Park to Embarcadero, in order, from memory. The
  team checks against the map. Any slip = new 60 seconds, new reciter. Complete on a
  clean run. (No fare needed — the map is outside the gates.)
- **Where:** Glen Park BART station, Diamond St & Bosworth St
- **Time:** 5–10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (re-study and retry)
- **Type:** Memory
- **Photo:** The reciter with their back to the map, mid-list, concrete overhead.
- **Why here:** A Clipper-tap territory race deserves one challenge in the
  architect-adored station the whole neighborhood is built around.

## Recommended trio

**2, 4, 6** — One in each of Glen Park's three worlds: race eucalyptus-leaf boats on
Islais Creek in the wild canyon, whisper a sentence up the Harry Street stairs to Billy
Goat Hill, and recite the BART stations to Embarcadero from memory at the village's
Brutalist station. A float game, a telephone game, a memory game; nothing failable, zero
strangers, all $0, ≈ 40–50 min. (The 60-second dynamite fuse and the Bird & Beckett dig
stay as alternates.)
