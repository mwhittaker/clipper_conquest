# Oceanview/Merced/Ingleside (OMI)

The OMI is San Francisco's sunny-name, foggy-reality southern edge: Ingleside Terraces
was built inside the oval of the old Ingleside Racetrack (Urbano Drive still traces the
track), and its developer planted a 28-foot sundial there in 1913 and called it the
world's biggest. Ocean Avenue is the commercial spine — the Art Deco El Rey Theater
tower, the K Ingleside streetcar, and Beep's Burgers slinging shakes since 1962 — while
Brooks Park hides a hilltop community garden with ocean views on Merced Heights. We
anchored on the racetrack/sundial oddities, the Ocean Ave strip, and the secret hill.

## Candidates

### 1. Shadow Forecast at the Giant Sundial
- **Do:** Find Sundial Park at the center of Entrada Court (inside the Urbano Drive
  oval): a 33-foot dial with a 28-foot gnomon. If the sun is out, mark where the tip of
  the gnomon's shadow falls right now (a shoe, a coin). Then the team agrees on a
  prediction: place a second marker where the shadow tip will be in exactly TEN minutes.
  Wait it out (phones away). Shadow tip within one hand-width of your marker = complete.
  Off? Re-mark and predict the next ten. Fogged in? Instead, guess the dial's diameter
  in paces, then pace it — within 10%.
- **Where:** Sundial Park, Entrada Court off Urbano Drive, Ingleside Terraces
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (re-predict, or take the fog option)
- **Type:** Call your shot
- **Photo:** Two markers and a shadow tip, gnomon looming behind.
- **Why here:** A 1913 "world's biggest sundial" hidden in one of SF's foggiest
  neighborhoods — the joke writes itself, and the shadow moves faster than you think.

### 2. Parked Cars on the Ghost Track
- **Do:** Urbano Drive is an oval because it sits exactly on the old Ingleside
  Racetrack, the last horse-racing venue in San Francisco. At the Entrada Court entrance,
  before anyone looks down the street, the team agrees on ONE guess: how many cars are
  parked along the OUTER curb of the whole oval right now? Write it down, then walk the
  full lap counting together. Within 10% = complete. Off? One more guess — the inner curb — and
  walk the lap again (about a mile). Miss twice = failed.
- **Where:** Urbano Drive oval, Ingleside Terraces; start and finish at the Entrada
  Court entrance (Sundial Park is inside it).
- **Time:** 15–20 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** retryable (two guesses, two laps)
- **Type:** Guess, then measure
- **Photo:** The guess and the count held up at the Entrada Court gate, the curve of the
  old track behind.
- **Why here:** You are literally pacing the footprint of a 19th-century racetrack; the
  street's shape is the proof, and its curbs never stop changing.

### 3. Milkshake at the 1962 Burger Beacon
- **Do:** Go to Beep's Burgers, the retro drive-in-style burger stand feeding Ocean
  Avenue since 1962 (open weekend daytimes: Sat from 10am, Sun from 11am). Buy ONE
  milkshake (their chocolate shake is locally famous). Every teammate must take a sip.
  Complete when the shake is bought and everyone has sipped.
- **Where:** Beep's Burgers, 1051 Ocean Ave (near Frida Kahlo Way / City College)
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** ~$8
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Eat & buy
- **Photo:** the whole team crowded around one shake under Beep's vintage red sign
- **Why here:** A 60-plus-year-old walk-up burger stand is the Ocean Avenue institution
  — teens line up for these shakes.

### 4. Streetcar Under the Deco Tower
- **Do:** The El Rey Theater at 1970 Ocean Ave is an Art Deco movie palace with a
  stepped 150-foot tower (a city landmark; the tower once carried an aircraft beacon).
  The K Ingleside streetcar runs right down Ocean Avenue in front of it. Capture ONE
  photo that contains both the full El Rey tower (top included) and a K streetcar
  passing. Trains come every 10–15 minutes on weekends, so scout your camera angle
  while you wait. Shoot from the sidewalk — never from the trackway.
- **Where:** El Rey Theater, 1970 Ocean Ave (at Fairfield Way)
- **Time:** 5–20 min (depends on the next train) | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (another train always comes)
- **Type:** Frame the shot
- **Photo:** the shot itself — streetcar and 150-foot Deco tower in a single frame,
  team optional in foreground
- **Why here:** Nowhere else in SF does a streetcar glide past a neighborhood Art Deco
  cinema tower like this.

### 5. Summit the Secret Farm Hill
- **Do:** Climb to Brooks Park (entrance at 400 Shields St), the hilltop park on
  Merced Heights with a ~90-plot community garden and views to the Pacific. From the
  public paths, find and photograph 4 DIFFERENT food plants actually growing
  (vegetables, fruits, or herbs) and name each one in your claim. Finish with a team
  photo at the hilltop. Complete = 4 named plant photos + summit team photo.
- **Where:** Brooks Park, 400 Shields St (at Victoria Ave), Merced Heights
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** team at the hilltop garden with the best plant find held up for the
  camera (not picked — pointed at!), ocean haze behind
- **Why here:** A working farm-garden on one of SF's southwesternmost hilltops — the
  "Oceanview" view most San Franciscans have never seen.

### 6. Build a Sundial on the Sundial
- **Do:** Outside the fence of Sundial Park's giant dial, build your own: a stick or
  pencil stood upright in the grass or a dirt patch, a ring of pebbles for hour marks.
  Set it so it reads the CURRENT time within 30 minutes — no compass apps, no peeking at
  the big dial for the shadow angle. Check against a phone. Complete when it reads
  right. Fog? Build a pebble cairn instead: seven stones stacked, standing for ten
  counted seconds.
- **Where:** Sundial Park lawn, Entrada Court, Ingleside Terraces
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (adjust the stick until it reads)
- **Type:** Build & make
- **Photo:** Your six-inch sundial with the 33-foot one behind it, both casting shadows.
- **Why here:** Joseph Leonard built the neighborhood's landmark sundial as a 1913
  publicity stunt; a pencil and seven pebbles can do the same job.

## Recommended trio

**2, 4, 6** — Wager on the parked-car count around the ghost racetrack of Urbano
Drive, time a K streetcar under the El Rey's Art Deco tower in one frame, and build a
pencil-and-pebble sundial that actually tells time. An estimation game, a timing shot, a
build; nothing failable, zero strangers, all $0, ≈ 40–50 minutes.
