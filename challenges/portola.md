# Portola

SF's official Garden District: a century ago ~20 family nurseries here grew most of the
city's flowers, and the last redwood-and-glass greenhouses of the "Rose Factory" still
stand in ruins at 770 Woolsey. Today the neighborhood pours that heritage into art — a
garden-mural trail along the San Bruno Avenue dim-sum-and-produce strip, and The Green
Between, a neighbor-built greenway on old Caltrans land complete with a Gothic gate
welded from garden tools and a half-buried 1969 Dodge. I anchored on the greenway, the
ghost greenhouses, and the strip.

## Candidates

### 1. The Gate Made of Garden Tools
- **Do:** Enter The Green Between greenway and find artist Dana Albany's 16-foot Gothic
  metal gate, welded entirely from old tools and hardware. In the gate itself, locate
  all four of: a hammer, a faucet, a gear, and any garden tool (trowel, shears, rake...).
  Then walk the greenway until you find the car half-buried nose-down in the ground (a
  1969 Dodge Coronet). Complete when you've photographed both.
- **Where:** The Green Between — the greenway strip between San Bruno Ave and the 101
  sound wall; enter at the Burrows St end (Burrows St just east of San Bruno Ave).
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** Team posed with the half-buried Dodge, plus one shot of a teammate's finger
  on each of the four objects found in the gate (a 4-tile collage counts).
- **Why here:** A cathedral gate built from the Garden District's own tools, on a park
  the neighbors built themselves — it exists nowhere else in SF.

### 2. Birds on the Ghost Greenhouses
- **Do:** San Francisco's last surviving commercial greenhouses — the 1922 Garibaldi
  family "Rose Factory" — stand as beautiful ruins behind a fence on Woolsey St. From the
  sidewalk, before anyone studies the frames, the team agrees on ONE guess: how many
  birds will land on or fly through the greenhouse frames in the next FIVE minutes?
  Write it down, start the clock, and count together (do not enter the fenced site).
  Within 2 = complete. Off? Call the next five minutes.
- **Where:** 770 Woolsey St (the full block around Woolsey at Bowdoin St, University
  Mound); pick the sidewalk with the clearest sightline through the fence.
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (call the next five minutes)
- **Type:** Call your shot
- **Photo:** The guess and tally with the redwood-and-glass frames behind the fence.
- **Why here:** This one block IS why Portola is the Garden District; the ruins are its
  most astonishing "wait, that exists?" sight, and the birds have moved in.

### 3. Garden District Blind Touch
- **Do:** At any market on San Bruno Ave (San Bruno Market, 2480 San Bruno, is the
  classic), one teammate — the shopper — goes in alone, counts along the produce display, and buys whatever sits in the 3rd,
  8th and 13th bins (skip a repeat or anything over $2; take the next bin), $5 total
  max, keeping them hidden in the bag. Outside, a teammate
  closes their eyes and is handed the items one at a time: they may touch, squeeze and
  smell, but not look or taste. Name all three correctly = complete. Any miss = the
  shopper reshuffles and a new teammate takes the test — one attempt per teammate. When
  everyone has missed, the shopper buys three new items and the round starts over. Eat
  the evidence afterward.
- **Where:** San Bruno Ave commercial strip (2400–2600 blocks).
- **Time:** ~10 min | **Cost:** ~$3–5
- **Failable:** no (one attempt per teammate per bag; a new bag costs money)
- **Type:** Blind senses
- **Photo:** An eyes-closed teammate mid-squeeze of a mystery vegetable; bonus style if
  the "Welcome to the Portola — Garden District" sign (San Bruno & Silliman) is behind.
- **Why here:** The Portola literally fed and flowered SF — identifying its produce
  blind is the neighborhood's own quiz.

### 4. Painted to Real
- **Do:** San Bruno Ave wears its garden history on its walls: "Portola Gardens" is
  painted on San Bruno Market (2480 San Bruno) and "Portola Blossoms" on the Hawaiian
  Drive-Inn (2600 San Bruno). Pick any fruit, vegetable or flower painted in either
  mural, then find the REAL thing for sale inside a shop on the same street and
  photograph the two side by side (painted on one phone, real in hand). Complete with
  two matches from two different murals. Buying is optional.
- **Where:** 2444–2600 San Bruno Ave (about 3 blocks).
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0 (optional produce)
- **Failable:** no (keep matching)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** A painted tomato beside a real one.
- **Why here:** These murals were painted to tell Portola's nursery history; the
  produce markets on the same block still sell the subject matter.

### 5. Snake at the Gateway
- **Do:** Hidden somewhere in the mural on the freeway-gateway art island is the
  endangered San Francisco garter snake — often called the most beautiful snake in North
  America — tucked into the painted grass among flowers and butterflies. Find it and
  photograph a teammate pointing right at it. No hints from anyone who's already seen it.
- **Where:** The Portola's freeway-gateway art island (support pillars by the 101
  underpass at San Bruno Ave).
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** A finger on the painted snake, butterflies all around it.
- **Why here:** An endangered local snake hiding in a mural at the neighborhood's front
  door — a real-life "Where's Waldo."

### 6. Bottle Flip Under the Marquee
- **Do:** Stand beneath the restored neon marquee of the 1927 Art Deco Avenue Theater —
  the neighborhood's old movie palace, whose sign (99 pieces of glass) neighbors fought
  years to relight. Flip a partly-filled water bottle (any size, about a third full) and
  land it standing upright on the sidewalk. Every teammate must land one. A miss = flip
  again; unlimited tries.
- **Where:** Avenue Theater, 2650 San Bruno Ave (sidewalk).
- **Time:** 5–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (flip until it stands)
- **Type:** Aim & throw
- **Photo:** The bottle standing, "AVENUE" glowing above it.
- **Why here:** The Avenue is San Bruno Ave's Art Deco crown jewel — landed under lights,
  like a premiere.

## Recommended trio

**1, 3, 6** — Find a hammer, faucet, gear and garden tool welded into the Gothic tool
gate (plus the half-buried car), name three San Bruno Ave produce items by touch alone,
and land a bottle flip under the Avenue Theater's neon. A find, a blind-touch game, a
skill toss; nothing failable, zero strangers, ~$3–5, ≈ 35–45 minutes. (The garter
snake hidden in the gateway mural stays the alternate find.)
