# Pacific Heights

San Francisco's mansion district: a ridge of Gilded Age palaces, two hilltop parks with
knockout bay views, and the city's most famous residential staircase, all bookended by
the boutiques of upper Fillmore Street. This is where movies come to film rich-people SF
— the Mrs. Doubtfire house, the What's Up, Doc? stair chase — so the challenges lean on
climbing, movie lore, and gawking-from-the-sidewalk (every mansion here is a private
home; everything below stays on public sidewalks and park paths). Anchors: the Lyon
Street Steps, Alta Plaza and Lafayette parks, outer Broadway's "Gold Coast," and
Fillmore between Jackson and California.

## Candidates

### 1. The Stair Oracle of Lyon Street
- **Do:** Pick any one step of the Lyon Street Steps and stand where you can watch it.
  Before you start, the team agrees on a guess: how many people will set foot on that
  step in the next five minutes? Write the guess down and photograph it, then start a
  five-minute timer and count every person (teammates don't count). Complete when your
  guess is within 3 of the true count (or within 10%, whichever is more generous).
  Missed? Pick a new number — or a new step — and run another five minutes; you know
  the traffic now.
- **Where:** Lyon Street Steps, Lyon St between Green St (bottom) and Broadway (top)
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (retry as many five-minute rounds as you like)
- **Type:** Call your shot
- **Photo:** The guess note held up next to the final tally, the chosen step behind you,
  with the Palace of Fine Arts dome and the bay in the distance — bonus points (moral
  only) for the heart sculpture in the stairway garden if it's in residence.
- **Why here:** SF's most manicured staircase — roughly 300 steps of hedges and bay
  views on the neighborhood's western edge, the local fitness pilgrimage — is also one
  of the city's busiest, so the count is a real game.

### 2. Count the Chase Steps
- **Do:** Alta Plaza Park's grand terraced staircase on the Clay Street side (at Pierce)
  is the one four cars famously crashed down in the 1972 *What's Up, Doc?* chase, filmed
  without permission. Count it: two teammates are the counters. They climb from the
  sidewalk to the top terrace at the same time, each counting every step silently — no
  talking, no fingers. At the top, both say their number at the same instant. A match =
  complete. A mismatch = recount together on the way down and say your numbers again
  at the bottom. (The chipped step edges the stunt cars left are still there — spotting
  one is bragging rights, not required.)
- **Where:** Alta Plaza Park, south stairs at Clay St & Pierce St
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (recount up or down until the two counters agree)
- **Type:** Mind meld
- **Photo:** The two counters holding up matching numbers on the top terrace, downtown
  skyline behind.
- **Why here:** The stunt that damaged these exact steps changed SF film-permitting
  forever — and the staircase is just long enough that two counts rarely agree the
  first time.

### 3. Pace the Great Hedge
- **Do:** Stand at the summit of Lafayette Park (the hilltop lawn) and look across
  Washington St at the towering street-side hedge hiding the white Spreckels Mansion
  (2080 Washington St). Before anyone moves, the team agrees on ONE guess: how many of
  your tallest teammate's paces will it take to walk from this spot to touch the hedge?
  Write it down. Then that teammate walks it, normal stride, everyone counting out loud,
  by the park paths and the crosswalk — no shortcuts across flowerbeds. Within 10% of
  the guess = complete. Off? Pick a new target visible from the hedge (a lamppost, the
  park gate, a tree) and pace that one.
- **Where:** Lafayette Park summit (enter near Washington & Gough/Octavia), then the
  sidewalk at the Spreckels Mansion hedge on Washington St
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (new target until a guess lands)
- **Type:** Guess, then measure
- **Photo:** The pacer's hand on the hedge, the guess and the real count held up
  side by side.
- **Why here:** SF's most gossiped-about hedge hides its grandest mansion, directly
  across from the neighborhood's summit park — the perfect ruler.

### 4. Price Ladder on Fillmore
- **Do:** Inside any one shop on Upper Fillmore (Mollie Stone's at 2435 California St
  is the easy pick; a toy or gift shop works too), one teammate — the shopper — quietly
  gathers FIVE different items, hiding their price tags, and sets them in a row. The
  team must arrange them from cheapest to priciest. Lock in the order, then the shopper
  reveals the prices. All five in the right order = complete. Any swap = the shopper
  returns them and picks five new items (buy a snack on the way out if you like — not
  required). Be tidy and quick; put everything back where it came from.
- **Where:** Upper Fillmore St shops, roughly Jackson St to Pine St (Mollie Stone's is
  at California & Fillmore).
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0 (optional snack)
- **Failable:** no (five new items until the ladder is right)
- **Type:** Guess, then measure
- **Photo:** The five items in their final order, tags revealed, team behind.
- **Why here:** Upper Fillmore is the neighborhood's main street, and its price tags
  are Pacific Heights' best-kept joke — guess which is the $14 candle.

### 5. Five-Word Mansion on the Gold Coast
- **Do:** Walk the 2800 block of Broadway (between Broderick and Baker), the heart of
  Billionaires' Row. One teammate, the describer, secretly picks any house on the block
  and describes it in EXACTLY five words — no address numbers, no colors of the front
  door read from the sidewalk plaque, just five words. The team points at the house on
  their first pick. Right = the next teammate describes. Wrong = new house, streak
  restarts. Complete when the team gets three in a row. Sidewalk only; every one of
  these is a private home.
- **Where:** Broadway, 2800 block (Broderick St to Baker St), the outer Broadway "Gold
  Coast"
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (the streak restarts until you hit three)
- **Type:** Secret message
- **Photo:** The describer's five words on a phone held up in front of the found house.
- **Why here:** The most expensive block of houses in the city, and every one is
  trying to look different — five words should do it.

### 6. Cast a Spell at the Witch's House
- **Do:** Find the gray 1886 Queen Anne Victorian at 2007 Franklin St — the
  Haas–Lilienthal House, with a corner tower topped by a pointed "witch's cap" roof.
  Under the turret, hold a spelling bee: one teammate opens any random-word generator
  on a phone (search "random word generator"), reads the word aloud — and its meaning,
  if nobody knows it — and the speller casts the spell by spelling it out loud, no
  peeking. Spelled right = the next teammate steps up with a fresh random word. Spelled
  wrong = same speller, new word. Complete when every teammate has cast one spell.
- **Where:** 2007 Franklin St (between Washington & Jackson) — sidewalk only, no tour
  required
- **Time:** 5–10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (new word until it's spelled)
- **Type:** Word game
- **Photo:** The speller mid-incantation under the witch's cap, the word on the phone
  held up beside them.
- **Why here:** The only intact Victorian home in SF open as a museum, wearing a
  witch's hat — the one place in the city where a spell has to be spelled.

## Recommended trio

**1, 2, 6** — Predict the foot traffic on one step of the Lyon Street Steps (guess,
count five minutes, retry until you nail it), send two counters up the *What's Up,
Doc?* staircase at Alta Plaza until their numbers match, and spell random words under
the witch's cap of the Haas–Lilienthal House. An estimation game, an agreement game, a
spelling bee; nothing failable, zero strangers, all $0, ≈ 30–40 minutes. (The Fillmore
price ladder stays the alternate.)
