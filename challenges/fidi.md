# Financial District/South Beach

Downtown's big east wedge: the Ferry Building and the Embarcadero promenade, the skyscraper
canyon, Salesforce Park floating 70 feet above the bus deck, South Park's old oval — and yes,
**Union Square is inside this analysis neighborhood** (its census tract 117 is officially
assigned here, verified against DataSF's tract-to-neighborhood table). Equally verified OUT:
the Transamerica Pyramid and Jackson Square's 1850s block sit in tract 611.01, assigned to
*Chinatown*, and Oracle Park's tract 607.01 belongs to *Mission Bay* — so I anchored nothing
on them. Offices are ghosts on weekends, so every candidate leans on the waterfront, parks,
monuments, and the Ferry Building, which is at its liveliest on a Saturday.

## Candidates

### 1. The Blindfold Market
- **Do:** Pick a taster. The taster waits OUTSIDE the Ferry
  Building, eyes closed, while a teammate goes in and buys one bite-sized edible thing
  ($10 max) from any shop or Saturday farmers-market stall — no hints, wrapper hidden.
  Blindfold the taster (hands over eyes works) and feed them one bite. On camera, they
  declare what they think it is. Then the blindfold comes off and the taster leads the
  team inside: they must find the exact shop it came from and point at its sign. They get
  TWO shop guesses. Both wrong = attempt failed; a retry requires a fresh purchase and,
  if possible, a fresh taster. You may not practice.
- **Where:** Ferry Building Marketplace, foot of Market St at The Embarcadero.
- **Time:** 15–20 min | **Cost:** ~$5–10 per attempt
- **Failable:** retryable (one purchase = one attempt, two guesses per attempt)
- **Type:** Blind senses
- **Photo:** The blindfolded tasting under the clock tower, then the triumphant point at
  the shop sign.
- **Why here:** The 1898 ferry landmark's nave holds forty-odd food vendors and is the one
  downtown spot MORE alive on weekends — enough options to make taste-and-seek a real
  deduction.

### 2. Mind the Doors
- **Do:** The F-line's historic streetcars — each one a rescued antique from a different
  city and decade — stop along The Embarcadero. Go to any F-line platform. You may watch
  streetcars arrive for as long as you like. Then declare your attempt: the whole team
  lines up shoulder-to-shoulder where you believe the FRONT DOOR of the next streetcar
  will be when it stops, and must be in position before the streetcar is visible.
  No moving once it appears. If any teammate lines up with any part of the open front
  door, you win. Miss = wait for the next streetcar to try again (they run every ~10
  minutes on weekends).
- **Where:** Any F Market & Wharves platform on The Embarcadero (Ferry Building stop is
  the classic).
- **Time:** 10–20 min | **Cost:** $0 (you never board)
- **Failable:** retryable (one streetcar = one attempt)
- **Type:** Call your shot
- **Photo:** The team flattened in a line on the platform, antique streetcar door gaping
  open exactly at (or hilariously far from) them.
- **Why here:** Every F car stops slightly differently because every F car IS different —
  a 1930s Milan tram doesn't brake like a 1950s Philadelphia PCC. Studying the fleet is
  the skill.

### 3. Heart Detective
- **Do:** Union Square keeps giant painted heart sculptures from the "Hearts in San
  Francisco" series stationed around the plaza, repainted by new artists each year. One
  teammate (the artist) slips away and photographs an extreme close-up — a palm-sized
  detail — of any one heart, any side. The rest of the team sees only that photo, then
  must find the spot: identify which heart, walk to it, and put a finger on the exact
  painted detail. First touch is the answer — right spot wins, wrong heart or wrong spot
  fails the attempt. Retry with a new close-up and a new artist. Artists may not answer
  questions.
- **Where:** Union Square plaza, Post/Stockton/Geary/Powell.
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** retryable (new close-up per attempt)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** Side-by-side keeper: the mystery close-up next to the finger-on-the-spot
  reveal, Dewey Monument in the background.
- **Why here:** "I Left My Heart in San Francisco" made these hearts the square's mascot —
  and Union Square really is inside this neighborhood's official boundary, which nobody
  on the other team will believe.

### 4. The Thirteen-Garden Memory Loop
- **Do:** Ride up to Salesforce Park, the 5.4-acre botanical garden floating on the
  transit center roof (free; gondola at Mission near Fremont when running, otherwise any
  open stair/elevator around the block). Walk the half-mile loop ONCE as a team — no
  photos, no notes: the gardens are labeled with signs. Then stop and, from memory, write
  down the names of at least EIGHT of the thirteen gardens. Verify by re-walking with the
  camera out. Eight matches = complete. Short? Walk it again and re-test — memory is
  allowed to improve.
- **Where:** Salesforce Park, atop the block bounded by Mission/Howard and Beale/Second.
- **Time:** 20 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (re-walk and re-test until you pass)
- **Type:** Memory
- **Photo:** The handwritten list beside a garden sign, Salesforce Tower shooting straight
  up behind the team on the central lawn.
- **Why here:** Thirteen labeled mini-ecosystems on a ROOF is the memory-game venue SF
  didn't know it built — and Ned Kahn's Bus Jet Fountain fires its 247 geysers whenever a
  bus rolls beneath you.

### 5. Parrot Patrol
- **Do:** Before entering Sue Bierman Park, the team locks in a written over/under call:
  will you photograph a wild red-masked parakeet within TEN minutes — yes or no? Then
  start the clock and hunt (listen for the squawking; that's your radar). Your call must
  be RIGHT to complete: photograph a parrot inside ten minutes after calling "yes", or
  survive the full ten minutes parrot-free after calling "no" (parrot photographed anyway
  = wrong call). Wrong call = wait 10 minutes, then re-call and retry.
- **Where:** Sue Bierman Park, Clay & Drumm Sts, just inland from the Ferry Building.
- **Time:** 10–20 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** retryable (one 10-minute window per call)
- **Type:** Call your shot
- **Photo:** A green parrot in frame with a teammate pointing at it — or ten smug minutes
  of parrotless sky if you called "no".
- **Why here:** The legendary wild parrots of downtown really do commute to this exact
  park — betting on birds is older than the Financial District itself.

### 6. The Human Machine
- **Do:** Find the Mechanics Monument — five giant bronze workers straining to drive a
  punch press. ONE teammate (the foreman) studies the statue; everyone else faces away
  and may never look at it. Using words only — no touching, no demonstrating — the
  foreman must direct at least three teammates into three DIFFERENT figures' poses. When
  the foreman calls "done," take the photo showing posers and statue together. The other
  team judges it at game's end: each poser must be clearly matchable to a distinct
  bronze figure. One rebuild allowed if the foreman isn't confident.
- **Where:** Mechanics Monument Plaza, Market/Bush/Battery.
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Secret message
- **Photo:** The team mid-strain beside the bronzes, directed entirely blind — an instant
  album cover with a story.
- **Why here:** Douglas Tilden's 1901 muscle-machine survived the 1906 quake and stands
  where the old waterfront ran before downtown was built on landfill.

## Recommended trio

**1, 2, 3** — The Blindfold Market, Mind the Doors, and Heart Detective: hidden-info
taste deduction, a called-shot timing game against a century of streetcar engineering,
and an observation hunt — three real games in the neighborhood's three worlds (Ferry
Building, Embarcadero platforms, Union Square). All three are retryable with genuine
stakes per attempt, none need strangers, everything runs weekend daytime, and total
on-site time is about 40–55 minutes. The Thirteen-Garden Memory Loop is the swap-in if
you want one guaranteed-completable anchor.
