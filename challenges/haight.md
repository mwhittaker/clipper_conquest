# Haight Ashbury

The official analysis neighborhood is bigger than the famous corner: it runs from
Stanyan/Golden Gate Park east past Divisadero, and includes the Panhandle, Buena Vista
Park (SF's oldest park, 1867), and Cole Valley. Anchors: the Summer of Love strip of
Haight Street (giant legs, tie-dye everywhere, Amoeba Music in an old bowling alley),
the rock-legend houses on the side streets, and Buena Vista Park's genuinely strange
hidden history. (Tank Hill and Kezar Stadium are just OUTSIDE this neighborhood — don't
use them.)

## Candidates

### 1. Five-Word Cover at Amoeba
- **Do:** Inside Amoeba Music (1855 Haight St, a converted bowling alley), one teammate
  — the describer — picks any single record from one bin section (the records between
  two divider cards), memorizes its cover, and slides it back. The describer then tells
  the team which section it's in and describes the cover in EXACTLY five words — no
  artist names, no titles, no words printed on the sleeve. The team flips through that
  section and must hold up the record on their first pick. Right = the next teammate
  describes. Wrong = new record, streak restarts. Complete when the team gets three in
  a row. Reshelve everything nicely; no purchase required.
- **Where:** Amoeba Music, 1855 Haight St at Stanyan. Open daily 11am–7pm.
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0 (optional: grab something from the bargain bin)
- **Failable:** no (the streak restarts until you hit three)
- **Type:** Secret message
- **Photo:** The found record held up next to the describer's five words typed on a
  phone, bins stretching away behind.
- **Why here:** One of the world's great record stores, with enough covers per bin to
  make five words genuinely hard.

### 2. Summer of Love Record Dig
- **Do:** Inside Amoeba Music (1855 Haight St, a converted bowling alley), dig through
  the bins and find one album by EACH of three bands who actually lived in this
  neighborhood in 1967: (a) Grateful Dead, (b) Janis Joplin / Big Brother and the
  Holding Company, (c) Jefferson Airplane. Vinyl or CD, new or used. Complete when the
  team photographs all three albums held up at once inside the store (no purchase
  required — reshelve them nicely).
- **Where:** Amoeba Music, 1855 Haight St at Stanyan. Open daily 11am–7pm.
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0 (optional: grab something from the bargain bin)
- **Failable:** no (Amoeba's stock of these three is effectively bottomless)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** Three psychedelic album covers fanned out in the aisles of one of the
  world's great record stores.
- **Why here:** All three bands lived within a few blocks of this store during the
  Summer of Love — this is a pilgrimage dig, not a random one.

### 3. Tombstone Gutter Hunt
- **Do:** In Buena Vista Park, the paved path gutters are built from broken marble
  headstones taken from SF's demolished Gold Rush-era cemeteries in the 1930s — most
  were laid face-down, but some engraving still shows. Walk the paths (the stretch above
  the tennis courts on the Buena Vista Ave West side is the jackpot zone) and find a
  gutter fragment with legible carved letters or numbers. Complete with a close-up photo
  of the engraving with a teammate's finger pointing at it.
- **Where:** Buena Vista Park; enter at Haight St & Buena Vista Ave East, or from Buena
  Vista Ave West near Central Ave for the tennis-court paths.
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (the fragments aren't going anywhere; keep looking)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** A finger pointing at 100-plus-year-old tombstone lettering hiding in plain
  sight in a park gutter.
- **Why here:** SF's oldest park recycled its dead — an Atlas Obscura-grade secret most
  locals walking these paths have never noticed.

### 4. Which House Was the Band's?
- **Do:** Somewhere on the 700 block of Ashbury St (between Waller and Frederick) is the
  Victorian where the Grateful Dead lived in 1966–68. Phones away, no numbers read
  aloud, and NO TALKING from the moment you turn onto the block. Walk it once in
  silence, then every teammate secretly writes down which house they
  think it is — by color and position ("the purple one, third from Waller on the left").
  Reveal: if everyone picked the SAME house, complete, whether or not you were right.
  Split decision = failed — one vote only. Either way, check the number (it's 710) for
  bragging rights. Private homes — stay on the sidewalk, keep it quiet.
- **Where:** Ashbury St between Waller and Frederick, one block uphill from the
  Haight/Ashbury corner.
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** one-shot (one silent vote)
- **Type:** Mind meld
- **Photo:** The ballots held up in front of the house the team agreed on.
- **Why here:** An ordinary-looking house on an ordinary block was the Grateful Dead's
  headquarters — does your team's instinct for "rock star house" agree?

### 5. Tie-Dye Window Safari
- **Do:** Walking Haight St between Stanyan and Central, photograph FIVE different
  storefronts that display tie-dye visible from the sidewalk (clothing, tapestries,
  flags, painted signs — anything rainbow-swirled counts; five different businesses
  required). Complete when you have five photos, each showing the tie-dye item with
  enough storefront to tell the shops apart.
- **Where:** Haight St corridor, Stanyan to Central (Love on Haight at 1400 Haight is a
  guaranteed freebie).
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (works even when shops are closed — windows never sleep)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** A five-shot rainbow of tie-dye storefronts.
- **Why here:** No other street in America still sells this much tie-dye per block;
  it's the neighborhood's uniform.

### 6. Creatures of the Evolutionary Rainbow
- **Do:** Find the Evolutionary Rainbow mural at the corner of Haight & Cole — first
  painted by Yana Zegri in 1967 and defended by the neighborhood ever since (when an
  owner painted over it in 1982, protests brought it back). The rainbow contains the
  story of evolution. Complete with a photo (or photos) of teammates pointing at THREE
  different creatures painted in the mural (e.g., a fish, a dinosaur, a bird — any three
  distinct animals).
- **Where:** Haight St & Cole St, on the building at the corner.
- **Time:** 5–10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** The team spread across a 1967 rainbow, each pointing at a different beast.
- **Why here:** It's an original, still-surviving Summer of Love artwork — painted the
  same year the hippies arrived, and older than every mural around it.

## Recommended trio

**1, 3, 4** — Five-word cover descriptions in Amoeba's bins, the tombstone gutter hunt
in Buena Vista Park, and a unanimous vote on which Ashbury Victorian was the Grateful
Dead's. A describe-and-find game, a hidden-history hunt, a mind meld; nothing failable,
zero strangers, all $0, only Amoeba depends on a venue (open 11–7 daily), ≈ 30–40
minutes.
