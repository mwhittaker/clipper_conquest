# Outer Richmond

Foggy avenues that end at the Pacific: the northern stretch of Ocean Beach, the
village-y Balboa Street theater block, and the whole Point Lobos headland — Cliff House,
Camera Obscura, Sutro Heights, AND the Sutro Baths ruins, all of which sit inside Outer
Richmond on the official Analysis Neighborhoods map (census tract 479.04; the Lincoln
Park neighborhood only begins east of Lands End, at the Legion of Honor/Eagle's Point).
Anchors: a ruined Victorian mega-bathhouse, a building shaped like a camera, stone lions
guarding a lost millionaire's garden, a 100-year-old movie palace, and the ghost of
Playland-at-the-Beach.

## Candidates

### 1. Step Inside a Giant Camera
- **Do:** At the Camera Obscura — a 1946 building literally shaped like a giant camera —
  pay admission (~$3/person, cash) and go inside. The whole team (or at least two
  teammates if it's crowded) must watch the live rotating image of Seal Rocks and the
  surf projected onto the parabolic viewing table. Complete when you've seen the image
  sweep past Seal Rocks.
- **Where:** 1096 Point Lobos Ave, on the terrace behind the Cliff House building.
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** ~$3/person (cash)
- **Failable:** no — but it's a weather-permitting venue (typically open daily incl.
  weekends 11am–5pm; call 415-750-0415). Confirm it's open before committing; if
  shuttered by fog/storm, pick a different challenge.
- **Type:** Go there
- **Photo:** Team posed in front of the camera-shaped building so the giant "CAMERA
  OBSCURA" lettering reads across the frame — you standing "inside a camera."
- **Why here:** A walk-in Victorian optical instrument on a cliff is the neighborhood's
  weirdest survivor — one of the last pieces of the old Cliff House amusement era.

### 2. Through the Sutro Tunnel
- **Do:** Descend to the Sutro Baths ruins (stairs from the Lands End/Merrie Way lot or
  the trail from Point Lobos Ave). From the interpretive signs, find and report two
  numbers: how many swimming pools the 1896 bathhouse had (seven — six saltwater, one
  freshwater) and how many swimmers it held. Then walk the whole team through the rock
  tunnel at the north end of the ruins to its wave-window openings. Stay on the trail
  and in the tunnel — never on the wet rocks by the water.
- **Where:** Sutro Baths ruins, Point Lobos Ave & Merrie Way (below Lands End Lookout).
- **Time:** 15–20 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** Team silhouetted in the tunnel mouth with the ocean crashing behind — one
  of the best natural photo frames in San Francisco.
- **Why here:** The ruins of the world's largest indoor swimming establishment are THE
  Outer Richmond landmark (and yes — the official map puts them in this neighborhood,
  not Lincoln Park).

### 3. Dig Your Own Sutro Bath
- **Do:** Sutro's baths are ruins — build the successor. On the sand at Kelly's Cove
  (the north end of Ocean Beach, below the Cliff House), dig a pool at least one
  arm-span across that visibly holds water (carry water up in hands/hats/a bottle —
  dig on damp sand well above the waterline; nobody enters the surf). Complete when the
  pool holds standing water with at least one teammate's bare feet in it.
- **Where:** Ocean Beach at Kelly's Cove — beach access at Balboa St & Great Highway,
  then walk north toward the Cliff House.
- **Time:** 15–20 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Build & make
- **Photo:** Team in swimmer poses around the filled "bath," with the Cliff House
  headland in the background — your bathhouse, with the real one's cliff behind it.
- **Why here:** Recreates the neighborhood's signature dead landmark at 1:1000 scale, on
  the beach it overlooked.

### 4. The Hidden Huntress
- **Do:** Enter Sutro Heights Park through the gate flanked by two stone lions (copies
  of Trafalgar Square's). Phones away: somewhere among the trees is a statue of Diana
  the Huntress (a concrete copy of the Louvre's Diana, bow in hand) — find her without a
  map. Then a memory test with three fixed questions: one teammate studies her for 30
  seconds, turns their back, and answers (1) which hand holds the bow, (2) which foot is
  forward, (3) what she is reaching for with her free hand. The team checks against the
  statue. Three for three = complete; any miss = 30 more seconds, new teammate. Finish at
  the parapet overlook above the Cliff House.
- **Where:** Sutro Heights Park, entrance at 48th Ave & Point Lobos Ave. Free, open
  dawn to dusk.
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (look again until three for three)
- **Type:** Memory
- **Photo:** The answerer with their back to Diana mid-answer, then the team at the
  parapet with the ocean behind.
- **Why here:** Adolph Sutro's clifftop estate is Outer Richmond's secret garden; the
  lions and Diana are the only statues that survived the 1939 demolition.

### 5. Marquee Scrabble at the Balboa
- **Do:** The Balboa Theatre opened in 1926 and turns 100 this year. Stand under its
  marquee and use ONLY the letters currently showing on it (movie titles and showtimes)
  — each letter as many times as it appears up there, no more — to spell one English
  word of at least SEVEN letters. Write it down and photograph it next to the marquee.
  Complete when a seven-letter word is spelled from marquee letters only.
- **Where:** Balboa Theatre, 3630 Balboa St (between 37th & 38th Ave), the heart of the
  Balboa Village shopping block.
- **Time:** 5–10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (keep rearranging)
- **Type:** Word game
- **Photo:** The word written on a phone, held up under the marquee it came from.
- **Why here:** A neighborhood movie palace hitting exactly 100 years old in 2026 — its
  marquee is the block's biggest word puzzle.

### 6. Eat History: An It's-It Where It Was Born
- **Do:** The It's-It ice cream sandwich was invented in 1928 by George Whitney at
  Playland-at-the-Beach — the amusement park that ran along Ocean Beach between Balboa
  and Fulton until 1972 — and for 40+ years you could ONLY buy one there. Buy It's-Its
  (~$3 each, one to split per 2–3 players is fine) at the Safeway at 850 La Playa St, a
  block from the old Playland grounds, then walk to the Great Highway sidewalk between
  Balboa and Fulton — Playland's actual footprint — and have every teammate take a bite
  standing where the park stood.
- **Where:** Safeway, 850 La Playa St (at Fulton), then Great Highway between Balboa &
  Fulton. Grocery store: reliably open all weekend, all day.
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** ~$3–6
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Eat & buy
- **Photo:** Team mid-bite, wrappers up, with the Great Highway/Ocean Beach behind —
  eating a 1928 invention on the spot it was invented.
- **Why here:** Playland's rides are gone, but its dessert never left San Francisco;
  this is the only neighborhood where eating one is a history lesson.

## Recommended trio

**2, 3, 4** — Walk through the rock tunnel at the Sutro Baths ruins (the trio's "the
place is the game" entry), dig a Sutro bath of your own on Ocean Beach that actually
holds water, and find the hidden Diana in Sutro Heights, then answer three fixed
questions about her from memory. One ruin, one build, one hunt-and-remember; nothing
failable, zero strangers, all $0, ≈ 40–50 minutes.
