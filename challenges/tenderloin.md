# Tenderloin

Downtown's most storied thirty-odd blocks: a National Register historic district where
400-plus early-1900s residential hotels stack bay windows, fire escapes, and vintage
blade signs, Little Saigon's banh mi counters line Larkin Street, and the Market Street
edge glitters with 1920s theater-palace marquees. Boundary verified against the official
Analysis Neighborhoods dataset: Market Street north to roughly Post/Sutter, Powell west
to Van Ness — so UN Plaza, the Golden Gate Theatre, the Warfield, and the Orpheum are all
in-bounds, while Union Square plaza itself belongs to a neighbor. Everything below sticks
to the neighborhood's busy, daytime-comfortable corridors: Larkin, O'Farrell/Geary, the
Market edge, and UN Plaza.

### 1. The Six-Dollar Legend
- **Do:** Buy one banh mi at Saigon Sandwich (560 Larkin St at Eddy) — or, if the line
  is out the door, at any other banh mi counter on Larkin between Turk and O'Farrell.
  Every teammate takes at least one bite. Complete when the sandwich is bought and
  everyone has bitten.
- **Where:** Larkin St, Eddy to O'Farrell (officially designated Little Saigon); Saigon
  Sandwich is open daily, weekends from 7am
- **Time:** 10–15 min (there's often a line) | **Cost:** ~$6
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Eat & buy
- **Photo:** The whole team mid-bite on the Larkin sidewalk, Vietnamese storefront
  signage behind.
- **Why here:** Larkin between Turk and O'Farrell is San Francisco's official Little
  Saigon, and this closet-sized counter has sold arguably the city's best cheap lunch
  for decades — around $6, wrapped in paper.

### 2. Double Feature
- **Do:** Strike a full-team showbiz pose (jazz hands, red-carpet wave, or dramatic bow)
  under the Golden Gate Theatre's corner marquee at Golden Gate Ave/Taylor/Market, then
  walk one block east on Market and repeat a DIFFERENT pose under the Warfield's marquee
  at 982 Market. Complete when you have both photos, each showing the marquee or vertical
  blade sign plus the pose.
- **Where:** Market St's theater edge: Golden Gate Theatre (1 Taylor St) and the
  Warfield (982 Market St), one block apart
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Performance
- **Photo:** A two-frame set: jazz hands under "GOLDEN GATE," second pose under the
  Warfield sign.
- **Why here:** Two rival 1922 vaudeville palaces a block apart — the Tenderloin's
  Market Street edge was the West Coast's Broadway, and the marquees still blaze.

### 3. Blade Sign Alphabet
- **Do:** The Uptown Tenderloin Historic District keeps the West's densest collection
  of century-old hotel signs. Photograph five vintage hotel signs — neon blades or
  painted ghost signs, five different buildings — whose hotel names begin with FIVE
  DIFFERENT letters. No phone research or maps; eyes up, feet moving. Complete at the
  fifth letter.
- **Where:** Richest hunting along Geary, O'Farrell, and Eddy between Larkin and
  Taylor; the Cadillac Hotel (398 Eddy) is a classic opener
- **Time:** 15–25 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** Sign #5 with a teammate pointing up, plus the full five-letter set in
  the upload.
- **Why here:** The neighborhood's skyline is spelled in hundred-year-old neon —
  working the alphabet through it is the Tenderloin's own crossword.

### 4. Memorize the Nations
- **Do:** UN Plaza's light-standard columns carry the names of all UN member nations
  (the Charter was signed in SF in 1945). Pick one reciter and one verifier. The
  reciter chooses any single column and any run of SEVEN CONSECUTIVE country names on
  it, studies as long as they like, then walks to the brass "WE THE PEOPLES" preamble
  in the pavers — back to the columns — and recites the seven IN ORDER, no peeking.
  The verifier stands at the column and checks every name against the inscription.
  Any wrong country, wrong order, or blank = stop, go re-study, and restart the
  recitation from name one. Complete on a clean seven.
- **Where:** United Nations Plaza, off Market St between Leavenworth and Hyde
- **Time:** 10–20 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (restart and re-study until the run is clean)
- **Type:** Memory
- **Photo:** The reciter mid-recital on the brass "WE THE PEOPLES," eyes shut, verifier
  at the column with a thumbs-up.
- **Why here:** The UN's San Francisco birthplace turned into a memory recital — world
  order, in order, from memory.

### 5. Sergeant Macaulay's Playground Passport
- **Do:** At Sergeant John Macaulay Park, every teammate goes down
  a slide once and crosses one piece of climbing equipment without touching the ground.
  Finish with the whole team jumping in the air at once in front of the play structure.
  Complete when everyone has slid, everyone has climbed, and the jump photo is taken.
- **Where:** Sgt. John Macaulay Park, Larkin St at O'Farrell
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Body
- **Photo:** The full-team mid-air jump, playground colors behind.
- **Why here:** A fenced pocket playground two blocks up Larkin from the banh mi
  counters — the Tenderloin's family backyard, and the perfect picnic spot if you pair
  it with candidate 1.

### 6. The Catalog Is You
- **Do:** Enter the Main Library (free, everyone welcome). From the door: all phones
  away, no catalog computers, no asking staff. Find a physical copy of Armistead
  Maupin's *Tales of the City* on the open shelves — any edition, and any volume of
  the series counts — using nothing but floor directories, shelf signage, and your
  own logic. Complete when a teammate is holding the book. (No copy on the shelf
  after an honest search of the right section? Any book by Maupin counts.)
- **Where:** SF Main Library, 100 Larkin St at Grove — one block from UN Plaza
  (weekends roughly 10–6 Sat, 12–6 Sun; check hours)
- **Time:** 10–20 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (the book is in there somewhere)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** The book held aloft under the library's 75-foot atrium skylight.
- **Why here:** The city's flagship library is officially IN the Tenderloin — six
  floors of it — and the most San Francisco novel ever written is hiding inside on
  a knowable shelf. Our homage to Jet Lag's Dewey Decimal challenge.

## Recommended trio

**1, 3, 6** — Banh mi in Little Saigon first (the trio's simple pleasure — around $6
of the city's best cheap lunch, fuel for what follows), then work the Blade Sign
Alphabet east through the historic district, and finish inside the Main Library —
officially Tenderloin turf — hunting down *Tales of the City* with no phones, no
catalog, no staff. One taste, one alphabet hunt, one shelf-logic quest; nothing
failable, zero strangers, all on the neighborhood's busiest daytime corridors,
≈ 40–55 minutes. Memorize the Nations is the swap-in if the library is closed.
