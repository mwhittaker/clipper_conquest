# Outer Mission

San Francisco's bottom edge: the Mission Street corridor rolling downhill to the Daly City
line, with Cayuga Terrace and the Crocker-Amazon blocks around it (the official Analysis
Neighborhood folds Crocker-Amazon and Mission Terrace in; note that Crocker Amazon
Playground itself sits in the McLaren Park polygon, so nothing here depends on it). It's a
working-class Filipino and Latino neighborhood with BART thundering overhead on elevated
tracks, a hidden forest of 375+ hand-carved folk-art sculptures by park gardener Demetrio
Braceros, a yard full of vintage streetcars, and the literal end of San Francisco.

## Candidates

### 1. Sculpture Safari in the Secret Forest
- **Do:** In Cayuga Park, find and photograph FIVE different hand-carved wooden
  sculptures by Demetrio Braceros — at least one animal and at least one person among
  them (he carved everyone from Adam and Eve to John Lennon).
- **Where:** Cayuga Park, Cayuga Ave at Naglee Ave.
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** Five carving shots; the keeper is a teammate beside their favorite.
- **Why here:** A retired gardener spent years carving a hidden forest of folk-art
  sculptures in a park under the BART tracks — nothing else like it exists in SF.

### 2. Call the Cars
- **Do:** Stand in Cayuga Park directly under the elevated BART tracks. Before each
  train, the team agrees on ONE call: how many cars will the next train have? (BART runs
  trains of different lengths.) Say it out loud on video, then count the cars as they
  thunder overhead. Exactly right = complete. Wrong = new call for the next train; they
  pass every 5–10 minutes on weekends, and every train teaches you something.
- **Where:** Cayuga Playground meadow/paths, Cayuga Ave & Naglee Ave, under the BART
  viaduct.
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (one call per train; keep calling)
- **Type:** Call your shot
- **Photo:** The team pointing up at the train mid-count, the viaduct filling the frame.
- **Why here:** Cayuga is the rare park built directly under BART's elevated tracks —
  the trains ARE the scenery, so make them the game.

### 3. Streetcar Time Machine
- **Do:** From the public sidewalk at Geneva & San Jose Aves, photograph BOTH (a) a
  vintage streetcar (old rounded PCC-style car, not a modern Muni train) parked in Muni's
  Cameron Beach Yard, and (b) the 1901 brick Geneva Car Barn & Powerhouse across from the
  yard. One photo or two; both targets must be identifiable.
- **Where:** Geneva Ave at San Jose Ave, beside Balboa Park BART. Muni stores its historic
  F-line streetcars in an open shelter here, visible from the sidewalk; the Powerhouse is
  the arched-window brick landmark on the corner.
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** Team pointing at a rainbow-liveried vintage streetcar through the fence, 1901
  powerhouse in frame.
- **Why here:** This corner has run streetcars since 1901 — a National Register powerhouse
  next to the shed where SF's antique fleet sleeps on weekends.

### 4. Pan Dulce at the End of the Line
- **Do:** Buy any sweet baked treat for under $5 from a bakery or donut shop on Mission
  St between Geneva Ave and the Daly City line (reliable weekend-daytime picks, side by
  side: Panadería Tikal at 5099 Mission, a Guatemalan bakery, and Western Donuts at 5101
  Mission, an old-school donut counter — both open early, daily). Every teammate must
  take a bite. Complete = purchase made + all-bites photo evidence.
- **Where:** Mission St, 5000–5900 blocks (Geneva Ave south to the county line).
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** ~$5
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Eat & buy
- **Photo:** The treat held up in front of the shop sign, then the team mid-bite.
- **Why here:** The Outer Mission's stretch of Mission St is a genuine everyday
  Latino/Filipino corridor — pan dulce and 5 a.m. donuts, zero tourists.

### 5. Find the Edge of San Francisco
- **Do:** Somewhere on Mission Street south of Geneva, San Francisco ends and Daly City
  begins — and the sidewalk mostly doesn't tell you. Phones away. Walk south and figure
  out, from clues alone (street-sign styles, house-number jumps, whose trash cans line
  the curb, any sign you spot), exactly where the city line crosses Mission Street. When
  the team agrees, plant a shoe on the line and take the photo. Then — and only then — check a map: within
  one block of the real line = complete. Off = failed; the map has spoiled it.
- **Where:** Mission St, 5000–5900 blocks (Geneva Ave south to the county line).
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** one-shot (one plant, then the map)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** A shoe on the guessed line, one teammate on each "side," the map check held
  up beside it.
- **Why here:** This neighborhood is where San Francisco literally ends; in a game about
  conquering SF territory, finding the last inch of the map by deduction is the point.

### 6. European Vacation, Crocker-Amazon Style
- **Do:** Collect street-blade signs for 4 different European cities WITHOUT leaving this
  neighborhood: photograph a teammate under 4 different street signs named for European
  cities, all at corners on or south of Geneva Ave and east of Mission St. Valid finds
  include Geneva, Naples, Munich, Prague, Cordova, Seville, and Florentine. (Geneva ×
  Naples, Geneva × Munich, and Geneva × Prague are consecutive corners — a tidy loop via
  Cordova St bags all four.)
- **Where:** Crocker-Amazon residential blocks around Geneva Ave between Naples and
  Prague Sts; the pocket park Naples Green (Naples/Rolph/Cordova) makes a bonus backdrop.
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** A 4-shot collage — one goofy "tourist arriving in this city" pose per sign.
- **Why here:** Crocker-Amazon's street grid is a pocket atlas of Europe; nowhere else in
  SF can you "visit" Geneva, Naples, Munich, and Prague in a 15-minute walk.

## Recommended trio

**1, 2, 5** — Hunt five hand-carved sculptures in Cayuga Park, call the car count of
the next BART train overhead, and deduce where San Francisco ends on Mission Street
without a map. One find, one prediction game, one deduction; nothing failable, zero
strangers, all $0, ≈ 40–50 minutes.
