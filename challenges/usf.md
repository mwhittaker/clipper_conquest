# Lone Mountain/USF

A cemetery hill that became a women's college that became the Jesuit hilltop campus of the
University of San Francisco. Everything here climbs: a 143-step grand staircase modeled on
Rome's Spanish Steps, the 210-foot twin spires of St. Ignatius Church (in 1914 the tallest
points in San Francisco, used by ship captains as a navigation landmark), and a summit with
one of the city's best hidden skyline views. I anchored on the staircase, the church, and
USF's surprisingly weird public art — a wolf statue, a 4,800-pound marble heart, medieval
Spanish archways, and a Qing-dynasty star machine — all outdoors and open to wanderers on
weekends.

## Candidates

### 1. Conquer the Spanish Steps of the West
- **Do:** Climb the Lone Mountain grand staircase from Turk Boulevard to the top — every
  teammate, no shortcuts. Pick two counters; each silently counts the steps on the way
  up. At the top, both write their number down hidden and reveal together. The counts
  must MATCH. Mismatch = walk back down and recount on the next climb. At the mid-landing,
  everyone roars at the gilded lion's-head spout of the Alessandri Fountain.
- **Where:** Lone Mountain grand staircase, from Turk Blvd up to the top terrace.
- **Time:** 10–20 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (recount until the counters agree)
- **Type:** Mind meld
- **Photo:** The two matching numbers held up at the top terrace, the city behind.
- **Why here:** SF's own Spanish Steps — and counting them while out of breath is harder
  than it sounds.

### 2. Run With the Wolves
- **Do:** Find Los Lobos de Loyola, the green-bronze statue of two wolves at a kettle in
  front of Gleeson Library. Recreate the sculpture: two teammates pose as the wolves
  (hands as paws, mouths open) on either side of a "kettle" you improvise from a backpack
  or jacket on the ground. The real statue must be fully visible in the same frame as your
  recreation. Complete when the photo is taken.
- **Where:** Gleeson Library plaza, USF main campus (enter from Fulton St at Parker Ave
  and head up into the campus core).
- **Time:** 5–10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Performance
- **Photo:** Statue wolves and human wolves side by side around their respective kettles.
- **Why here:** The wolves-and-kettle come from the Loyola family crest ("lobo y olla" —
  wolf and pot — is where the name Loyola comes from); its twin stands in Mexico City.

### 3. Pinch a Spire
- **Do:** St. Ignatius Church's twin spires rise 210 feet and once guided ships into the
  Bay. Take a forced-perspective photo in which one teammate appears to pinch the tip of
  a spire between thumb and forefinger — the pinch must look convincing (fingers visually
  touching the spire tip), and BOTH towers must be in the frame. Complete when you have
  the shot.
- **Where:** Sidewalks around St. Ignatius Church, Fulton St & Parker Ave (the Fulton
  Street side and the lawn edges give the best angles).
- **Time:** 5–10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (keep repositioning until the pinch lands)
- **Type:** Performance
- **Photo:** The forced-perspective spire pinch with both towers of the giant church.
- **Why here:** In 1914 these spires were the highest points in all of San Francisco —
  your fingers get to be higher.

### 4. Two Hearts of Lone Mountain
- **Do:** Find both of Lone Mountain's hearts. First: "Tides," the 4,800-pound heart-shaped
  marble sculpture by Yoko Kubrick installed near the top of Lone Mountain. Second: the
  heart-shaped Rock Garden along the URBAN Trail on the Lone Mountain slope, whose posted
  rule is "take a rock when you need some good news… then leave a rock to pass on good
  news." Follow the rule: take one rock, and leave one rock you picked up on your walk.
  Complete when you've photographed both hearts.
- **Where:** USF Lone Mountain campus — summit area for the marble heart; the URBAN Trail
  paths on the hillside for the rock garden.
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** A teammate making heart-hands framing the giant marble heart; second shot of
  your rock being left in the heart-shaped garden.
- **Why here:** A hilltop with a 2.4-ton marble heart and a DIY kindness rock garden is
  peak Lone Mountain — sweet, odd, and invisible from the street.

### 5. Walk Through the 1500s
- **Do:** USF's Kalmanovitz Hall is framed by two genuinely ancient doorways shipped from
  Europe: the Santa Maria de Ovila Portal (1575, from a Spanish monastery — it frames the
  outdoor auditorium stage) and a Romanesque portal from 12th–13th century northern Italy
  carved with Adam and Eve. Find both. One teammate stands framed inside the 1575 archway;
  at the older portal, a teammate points directly at the Adam and Eve carving. Complete
  when both photos are taken.
- **Where:** Kalmanovitz Hall and its outdoor terraces, USF main campus (uphill from
  Fulton St near Parker Ave).
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Go there
- **Photo:** Teammate posed in a 450-year-old stone archway; teammate pointing at an
  800-year-old Adam and Eve.
- **Why here:** Two of the oldest built structures in San Francisco are hiding on this
  campus, and almost nobody knows they exist.

### 6. The Emperor's Star Machine
- **Do:** Near USF's Lo Schiavo Science building sits a full-size bronze replica of the
  armillary sphere Jesuit missionary Ferdinand Verbiest built in the 1670s for the
  Chinese emperor. Find it and photograph a teammate sighting through its rings at the
  sky like a 17th-century astronomer.
- **Where:** USF main campus, by the Lo Schiavo Science building.
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** A teammate peering through the bronze rings.
- **Why here:** A replica of a Beijing observatory instrument hiding on a San Francisco
  campus is a genuine "wait, that exists?"

## Recommended trio

**1, 3, 5** — Count the Lone Mountain steps until two counters agree, pinch a St.
Ignatius spire in a forced-perspective skill shot, and walk through two genuinely
ancient European doorways at Kalmanovitz Hall. An agreement game, a photo skill shot,
a find; nothing failable, zero strangers, all $0, ≈ 35–45 minutes.
