# Lincoln Park

A tiny, dramatic neighborhood that is basically one clifftop park: the Legion of Honor
and its free outdoor Court of Honor (with a real Rodin *Thinker* and a mini Louvre
pyramid), the Lands End Coastal Trail with shipwreck and lighthouse sightlines, the USS
San Francisco Memorial built from the actual shell-torn bridge of a WWII cruiser, and a
golf course laid over a forgotten 19th-century cemetery. Boundary check against the
official Analysis Neighborhoods polygons: Sutro Baths, the Lands End Lookout visitor
center, and Sutro Heights all fall in **Outer Richmond**, not here — so everything below
anchors east of Merrie Way: the Legion, the memorial, Mile Rock overlook, Eagle's Point,
and the fairways.

## Candidates

### 1. Ships in the Gate
- **Do:** From the Eagle's Point overlook at the east end of the Coastal Trail, the
  Golden Gate is dead ahead. Before anyone starts watching the water, the team agrees on
  ONE guess: how many vessels of any size — freighters, ferries, sailboats, kayaks — will
  pass through the Gate (under or in line with the bridge) in the next TEN minutes?
  Write it down, start the clock, and count together. Within 1 of the true count =
  complete. Off? Call the next ten minutes.
- **Where:** Eagle's Point overlook, Coastal Trail trailhead at 32nd Ave & El Camino
  del Mar (a five-minute walk from the Legion of Honor)
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (call the next ten minutes)
- **Type:** Call your shot
- **Photo:** The guess and the tally held up with the bridge and a passing ship behind.
- **Why here:** The busiest stretch of water on the West Coast passes this clifftop —
  and nobody can look up how many boats the next ten minutes hold.

### 2. See the Ocean Through a Battle Scar
- **Do:** At the USS San Francisco Memorial, you're standing at the real bridge wings of
  the cruiser USS *San Francisco*, riddled with shell holes from the night Battle of
  Guadalcanal (Nov 1942). Two finds to complete: (a) locate a hole punched clean through
  the steel and photograph the Pacific or sky visible *through* it; (b) on the memorial's
  bronze plaques, find and photograph the name of Admiral Daniel J. Callaghan, who was
  lost on that bridge and received the Medal of Honor. Bonus fact for the car ride: the
  memorial is aimed along the exact compass course from San Francisco to Guadalcanal.
- **Where:** USS San Francisco Memorial overlook, 2400 El Camino del Mar (west end,
  by the flagpole at the Lands End parking loop above the Coastal Trail).
- **Time:** 10 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** The ocean framed inside a jagged shell hole in the gray steel — plus the
  close-up of Callaghan's name.
- **Why here:** This isn't a statue about a ship; it IS the ship — battle damage you can
  put your eye to, perched over the sea it sailed from.

### 3. The Lighthouse That Lost Its Head
- **Do:** From the Mile Rock overlook on the Coastal Trail, spot Mile Rock Lighthouse
  offshore — the stubby tower whose top was sliced off in the 1960s and replaced with a
  helicopter pad. Get a zoomed photo clearly showing its flat helipad top, with one
  teammate pointing at it from the overlook railing in a second (or the same) shot.
  Low-tide bonus flourish: from the same stretch of trail, look down for the rusted
  engine blocks of the tankers *Lyman Stewart* (wrecked 1922) and *Frank H. Buck*
  (wrecked 1937) poking out of the surf.
- **Where:** Mile Rock overlook, Lands End Coastal Trail (the signed overlook west of
  the Mile Rock beach stairs, between the USS San Francisco Memorial and Eagle's Point).
- **Time:** 10–15 min incl. trail walk | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** Zoomed shot of the decapitated, helipad-topped lighthouse; teammate
  pointing seaward from the overlook.
- **Why here:** Only this clifftop gives you a lighthouse wearing a helipad and two
  dynamited shipwrecks in a single view — the graveyard of the Golden Gate.

### 4. Walk the Vanishing Labyrinth
- **Do:** From the Eagle's Point end of the Coastal Trail, take the signed spur path
  down toward Lands End Point — the rocky promontory with the Golden Gate Bridge dead
  ahead. Completion: the whole team reaches the flat clearing at the point. If the
  famous stone labyrinth is standing (artist Eduardo Aguilera first laid it in 2004;
  vandals keep scattering it and volunteers keep rebuilding it — it was back as of
  2025), one teammate must walk the full spiral to the center without cutting across
  the lines and pose there. Go slowly on the narrow
  spur, and skip the outermost rocks if it's wet.
- **Where:** Lands End Point spur, off the Coastal Trail just west of the Eagle's Point
  overlook (trail access at El Camino del Mar & 32nd Ave).
- **Time:** 15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (reaching the point can always be done; the labyrinth walk is the
  flourish, not the requirement)
- **Type:** Go there
- **Photo:** Team at the point — in the labyrinth's center if it exists that day — with
  the Golden Gate Bridge behind.
- **Why here:** A secret, semi-mythical rock labyrinth that exists only when someone has
  recently rebuilt it, on the best bridge-view promontory in the city.

### 5. The Cemetery Under the Fairways
- **Do:** Lincoln Park's golf course is built on top of City Cemetery (1870–1909), and
  exactly two monuments were left standing. Find the Kong Chow funerary altar (1893), a
  brick-and-stone Chinese memorial near the Hole 1 green — its inscription calls it a
  temporary resting place for travelers from Kong Chow before their bones returned home
  to China. Photograph the team beside it with the Chinese characters legible in frame.
  Stay on the paved cart path from the 34th Ave & Clement entrance, walk the edge, and
  wait for any golfers to play through before crossing near the green.
- **Where:** Lincoln Park Golf Course, near the Hole 1 green — short walk from the
  clubhouse entrance at 34th Ave & Clement St.
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** Team at the 1893 altar, inscription visible, fairway rolling away behind —
  a graveyard hiding in plain sight.
- **Why here:** Thousands of graves still lie beneath these fairways; this altar is one
  of only two markers the city forgot to remove, now SF's first archaeological landmark.

### 6. Lost Ball Hunt
- **Do:** Lincoln Park's golf course wraps around the Coastal Trail and the Legion of
  Honor, and it sheds golf balls into the rough along every public path. Walking ONLY
  the public trail and sidewalk edges (never onto the course), find one lost golf ball.
  Complete when a ball is in hand; keep it as your trophy.
- **Where:** Public paths along the course: the Coastal Trail east from Eagle's Point,
  Clement St, and the road up to the Legion.
- **Time:** 10–15 min | **Cost:** $0
- **Failable:** no (keep looking — they're everywhere)
- **Type:** Scavenger & find
- **Photo:** The found ball held up with a fairway and the bridge behind.
- **Why here:** The only golf course in SF built on a cemetery and a cliff — and the
  slice count is high.

## Recommended trio

**1, 2, 4** — Call the number of vessels through the Golden Gate in ten minutes from
Eagle's Point, find a shell hole and Admiral Callaghan's name at the USS San Francisco
Memorial, and walk the Coastal Trail spur to Lands End Point and its stone labyrinth. A
prediction, a find, a place; nothing failable, zero strangers, all $0, ≈ 40–50
minutes.
